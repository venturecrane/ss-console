An incident is anything that stops a client's Operator from doing its job, or makes it do something it should not. This page is the full procedure: how serious it is, who does each step, what the client hears and when, and the rule that limits what the Operator may do afterwards; [When Something Goes Wrong](/admin/playbook/when-something-goes-wrong) is the one-page version keyed to the emails you receive.

## Running an incident

"You" is the administrator; "an agent" is an AI agent you direct; the Captain is named where the decision is the Captain's ([Who Does What](/admin/playbook/who-does-what)).

1. **Classify** against the ladder below. **You**, on the agent's first findings. When in doubt between two severities, take the higher.
2. **Stabilize.** **An agent** does the work. For a down seat: `flyctl status`, then the deploy and rollback path ([Deployment & Release](/admin/playbook/deployment-release)); never root SSH on a live Machine, which crash-loops its bootstrap ([Disaster Recovery](/admin/playbook/disaster-recovery)). For behavior outside what the client authorized: pause the seat first, investigate second; the audit log is the record. **You** decide to pause; the agent runs `operator/bin/pause-customer.sh <slug> --reason "<text>"`, which halts the Operator while keeping its Machine running for diagnosis (`--resume` undoes it).
3. **Demote.** A SEV1 also lowers every routine involved to the bottom rung, per the pre-committed rule below. This happens during stabilization, not after the write-up. **You** confirm it is done; **an agent** makes the change.
4. **Communicate.** **You** send the first client message inside the window, saying what is known, what is being done, and when the next update comes, in plain language, with no hedging and no blame. Status updates are routine client emails and yours to send; anything beyond a status update (an apology that offers something, a credit, a change to what the Operator does) is a new commitment and goes to **the Captain** first.
5. **Track to resolution.** **You** keep the updates at the committed cadence. The incident is over when the client agrees it is.
6. **Record.** **An agent** writes a dated post-incident note in `docs/runbooks/operator/incidents/` from `_TEMPLATE.md` in that directory: what broke, how it was detected, the timeline as recorded, and what changed to prevent it recurring, with the pull request, issue or gate cited. Where a source does not establish a fact (detection-to-resolution time is the usual one) the note writes `not recorded` rather than a plausible number. Every note gets a class from the fixed set in that directory's `README.md` (`gate-regression`, `built-not-wired`, `gone-not-gone`, `identity`, `other`) and is indexed there, so the next reader can ask whether this shape has happened before; `tests/incidents-register.test.ts` blocks merge on a note missing from the index or classed outside the set.

## The ladder

ADR 0064 (Captain decision, 2026-07-04) set the service commitment as a severity ladder over detection, response and communication. No uptime percentage, no service credits at launch: a founder-led firm with continuous automated monitoring but business-hours people commits to what it can stand behind, in the same register as an employee's sick day. We fix it fast and communicate honestly.

| Severity | Definition | Response | Client communication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SEV1 | Operator down (heartbeat past threshold) or acted outside authorized entitlements | Work begins immediately on detection, any day | Within 24 hours; at least daily updates until resolved |
| SEV2 | Degraded: connector broken, skills failing, drafts not flowing, breaker tripped | Same business day | If client-visible; updates as facts change |
| SEV3 | Questions, cosmetic issues, configuration requests | Next business day | In the same thread |

Business hours are Monday through Friday, Arizona time. Security incidents carry their own notification windows: 24 hours when client data or access is affected, 72 hours for a platform-level incident, matching the standing commitment in the security overview (section 10) and the DPA template's section 6 standard term of 24 hours. Security incidents also follow the DPA: cooperation with the client's own notification duties, information as it becomes available, tracked to resolution (ADR 0064).

## The exposure ladder and the demotion rule

Severity governs how we respond to an incident. The **exposure ladder** governs how much a routine was allowed to do before one, and it is what bounds the damage: `docs/runbooks/operator/enable-gate-checklist.md`.

Each routine climbs three rungs, and each rung is claimed by a named record rather than a report: a shadow-firm run id (rehearsed), dated review-period observations (drafting on the client seat under human review), then the Captain's signed sign-off (acting on its own). A routine with an empty evidence slot is on the rung below, and nothing climbs by age.

The demotion rule is decided in advance, so it is not left to whoever is handling the incident:

> **Any SEV1 pauses the seat and demotes every routine involved to the bottom rung, and the routine stays there until BOTH the root cause has landed as a merged change with its own evidence AND the incident exists as a shadow-firm scenario observed to fail against the unfixed state before it passes against the fixed one.**

There is no restoring the prior rung, because the prior rung's evidence is exactly what the incident disproved. The routine climbs again from the bottom. Two clarifications that have each cost us once: a fix that closes the reported symptom does not count if the class of problem survives, and a safety gate switched back on in report-only mode does not restore a routine, because report-only is a temporary staging state, not a steady one.

One rule came out of the `gate-regression` incidents (2026-08-04, 2026-08-13, 2026-08-19): **a new refusal gate on an outbound path is done when its refusal shows in `send_refusals` on the heartbeat and the pager has been proven on a seat, not when its unit tests pass.** A gate that can refuse and a person who can learn that it refused are one deliverable.

## Escalation

Everything escalates to the Captain (scott@smd.services); operational alerts land at team@smd.services. There is no second tier at this stage, and this page says so rather than implying an on-call rotation that does not exist.

## How incidents are detected

Each of these emails team@smd.services; what each email means and who acts is in [The Administrator's Week](/admin/playbook/administrators-week).

| Detector | What it catches | How it reaches you |
|---|---|---|
| **Heartbeats** | Every seat reports about every 60 seconds into `fleet_status`; staleness shows on the admin fleet dashboard and the client portal's aliveness chip | The 2-minute pager (`ss-fleet-alerts`) emails when a seat goes red, and once more when it recovers, as a reply in the same thread. Worst case about 7 minutes from failure to email. A seat that has never reported is being built and never pages. |
| **Cost and safety breaker** | A seat that stopped itself (`HARD_STOP`) on a cost cap or another recorded condition; the reason and condition ride the heartbeat | The same pager. Two states only, OK and HARD_STOP (`docs/runbooks/operator/sticky-stop-clear.md`). |
| **Drift** | A seat running an image, `customer.yaml` or skills version other than the one authored for it | The same pager compares each seat's heartbeat with the newest completed release, the published R2 config and the seat's skills pointer; one alert per seat and kind, one recovery notice when it converges ([Deployment & Release](/admin/playbook/deployment-release)) |
| **The outside-in probe** | The website, admin console and portal unreachable from outside | The same Worker fetches `https://smd.services/api/health` every 2 minutes; three failures in a row open one `edge_down` alert, two good probes close it. About 8 minutes worst case. A green probe proves the website and its database answer, nothing about the seats. |
| **Shortfalls** | The Operator could not give someone what they asked: a limit, a failure nothing recovered, a partial filing | `[SMD Ops] SHORTFALL <seat>`, at most once per seat per 30 minutes. Refusals that are working as designed come in the Monday digest `[SMD Ops] Weekly: what the Operator refused`. Nothing is sent to the client. |
| **Request cards** | Every request a person at the client emails a seat, and above all one nobody answered | `first reply in N m`, `NO REPLY after N m` (the alarm, at 30 minutes), and job delivered / failed / held cards. Read recent cards with `.claude/bin/requests`. SMD only, never the client. |
| **New kind of ask** | A client asked for a kind of job the seat has never done | The job is held and `NEW KIND OF ASK, job waiting` is sent; an agent runs a proof and you accept it or decline ([lane-proof runbook](https://github.com/venturecrane/ss-console/blob/main/docs/runbooks/operator/lane-proof.md)) |
| **Proven delivery** | A job that claimed delivery but whose output failed its independent review or its read-back | A `failed` shortfall with a `proof:<code>`, so it arrives in the SHORTFALL email; an open one re-raises daily |
| **Sentry** | A new or regressed error in the Operator's code | `Sentry issue alert`, one email per Sentry issue per seat per day, re-sent at most every 6 hours (`src/pages/api/webhooks/sentry.ts`) |
| **Work health** | Stuck, held or silent work across every seat | The hourly triage report and ACTION pages ([Work Health](/admin/playbook/work-health)) |
| **Payments** | A failed monthly retainer charge | `Retainer payment failed`; the steps that follow are the Captain's ([Money](/admin/playbook/money)) |
| **Client reports** | Anything the client notices first | The portal's change-request path and direct email |

## History

How the detectors above came to be, kept because each entry records a failure that one of them now catches.

**2026-07-04, the pager (#1709).** ADR 0064 first admitted that a down Machine paged nobody. The same day, the `ss-fleet-alerts` Worker began evaluating `fleet_status` every 2 minutes and emailing on heartbeat-red (last heartbeat older than the period plus grace) and HARD_STOP transitions. It is edge-triggered: one alert when a seat goes red, one recovery notice when it comes back, silence in between. The Worker only observes and emails; the response stays human. Since 2026-10-05 the recovery notice is a reply to its alert (same subject, `In-Reply-To` the alert's Message-ID, stored in `fleet_alert_state.alert_message_id`), so one incident is one inbox conversation.

**2026-09-02, the breaker reduced to two states.** The old WARN and SOFT_STOP levels restricted nothing and only named a cause, which read as a brake that was holding. They were removed; stop thresholds were unchanged (ADR 0062 amendment).

**2026-09-11, the outside-in probe.** Everything else is read out of `fleet_status`, which the seats write through the web Worker, so a dead web Worker would freeze that table and page nobody. The probe needs 3 consecutive failures (a non-200, a body that is not `status: ok`, or no answer inside 10 seconds) to open and 2 consecutive good probes to close, so a flapping site pages once. Only `smd.services` carries the route: the admin and portal hosts answer `/api/health` with their sign-in redirect, and all three ride the same Worker, so a dead edge takes them down together. Counters live in `edge_poll_state` (migration 0115). The alert path was proven that day with a deliberate 404 target beside the real one: one page when it went down, one recovery notice when it came back; that proof also found migration 0115 had not admitted `edge_down` into the alert table's vocabulary, which 0116 fixed. A target removed from the list is retired on the next tick, its counters deleted and any open alert resolved with a notice.

**2026-10-05, Sentry repaired.** `/api/webhooks/sentry` writes a `source='sentry'` alert-sink row that the pager Worker emails. From 2026-09-14 to 2026-10-01 every delivery was refused (`missing_tenant_tag`, 19 of 19 in Sentry's delivery log): the integration sent `issue` payloads, which carry no tags, to a receiver that required one, so no Operator error reached anyone. An error the receiver cannot pin to one seat is now paged as `fleet`, and a second error on the same seat on the same day is its own row. The source is now a global Sentry alert rule whose `event_alert` payload carries the `tenant` tag, so `SENTRY_ISSUE_RESOURCE = "ignore"` in `wrangler.toml` drops the tagless `issue` payloads; set it back to `page` only if that rule is deleted.

**2026-10-05, shortfalls.** A seat could be green while telling a paralegal it could not read her scan, or while filing none of the 52 pages it was handed (2026-10-01; every one of those tool results scored "ok", so nothing paged and SMD found out by watching). Each seat now classifies such results and reports the trailing day's events on its heartbeat, from person-initiated work as well as routines. Four classes: `limit` (a size, page or allowance cap stopped the work), `failed` (a call failed and nothing in the session recovered it; a refusal the Operator then retried successfully is not counted), `partial` (the mail routine filed fewer pages than it received), and `not_allowed` (outside what the seat is entitled to do, which goes to the Monday digest and is read as either a scope conversation with the client or an entitlement set wrong). Events land in `operator_shortfalls` (migration 0120) keyed by the seat's own stable key, so one event is one row and one email. Codes are a closed vocabulary; no client text reaches the email. Whether a client should hear "SMD has been told" is held until this is tuned (Captain, 2026-10-05).

**2026-10-27, a shortfall says what to do.** An A&P page read only "job_status, failed, error", and the Captain could not tell what happened or what to do next. It was also a false page: a case-file delivery turn read its job with `casefile_job_status`, then asked the chronology-only `job_status`, which said "no such job", and the reply went out ten seconds later. Three changes. A generic `job_status` miss in a session where a lane's own `<lane>_job_status` answered is no longer an event. Each event carries `request_at` (migration 0143), the time the request it was serving reached the seat, and the email points at that request's card in team@, which is where who asked and what they wrote live; the text itself never enters D1 (ADR 0052). And each event in the email reads as four lines in Phoenix time: when, which request, what happened in a sentence (error codes such as `no_such_job` and `http_500` are put into words), and what to do, which always starts from whether the person got their answer.

**2026-10-07, request cards.** A shortfall is the Operator saying it could not; the harder case is a request nobody answered at all, which looks exactly like a quiet day. Each seat posts a card to `POST /api/internal/operator-request-card` for every request a person at the client emails it, and the web app emails it to team@ in plain text. Three kinds: `[SMD Ops] <seat> request: first reply in <N>m: <subject>`, `[SMD Ops] <seat> NO REPLY after <N>m: <subject>` and `[SMD Ops] <seat> <lane> job <state>: <subject>`. The first-reply card carries who asked, the subject, the opening of the reply, the matter, the tools it ran, and refusals and failures; the NO REPLY alarm fires when nothing answered within 30 minutes and no queued job or held reply accounts for it; a job card follows when the job it started is delivered, fails or is held. A reply that arrives after its alarm still gets its own card. The email is the only place the request's text lives: `operator_request_cards` (migration 0122) holds the send ledger and counts, never the sender, subject, reply or matter (ADR 0052). A card is marked sent only when Resend accepted it, and the seat retries a failed send for a day under the same key, so a retry is never a second email. `.claude/bin/requests` counts alarms with no later reply, which is the number to act on.

**2026-10-22, a queued job read as no job.** A request that queued a chronology produced a `replied` card whose only job field (`job_lane`) was empty, because that field fills when a job ends. A watch built on the reader reported "neither request started a job" while the reply to the firm said the chronology was queued. The card's tool list had said so all along, and the ledger dropped it. Migration 0134 records the lanes a request queued (`jobs_queued`), and the reader prints `QUEUED <lane>` on the reply line, `queued none`, or `queued ?` for a card recorded before the fix. Unknown is printed as unknown, never as none.

**2026-10-22, the firm answered a hold and nobody heard it.** The same incident as "the firm's answer to an identity hold" below, seen from the mailbox: when Chris Price answered "just leave it out", the Operator replied that it could not act on that from its mailbox and that the hold clears only when the release is recorded on the job, naming the attachment's internal tag and the page allowance on the way, and he forwarded the exchange to the Captain, "Operator is speaking over my head". Two defects beside the missing withhold path. First, the seat carded his follow-ups as ordinary `replied` cards (the Operator's words, not his), so SMD learned his decision an hour later from that forward. Second, the Operator improvised: the hold's fixed wording names the record by its file name from the matter's documents, and an attachment inside a saved email has none, so the skill left it nothing to say. Now a message a requester writes in the thread of a request whose job is held posts a `held_reply` card at once, carrying the requester's own opening words and the held job's id, emailed as `[SMD Ops] <seat> REQUESTER WROTE on held <lane> job` (migration 0136; `.claude/bin/requests` lists it as `REQUESTER WROTE`). SMD reads the words and records the decision on the seat; the Operator still cannot (2026-10-13 above). On the Operator's side the chronology skill gained an ANSWER mode with fixed wording for the two things a requester says on a held job, "which record is it?" and the answer itself, and the hold question names an emailed attachment as "a record attached to one of the emails saved in the matter" when the matter's documents cannot name it. Nothing in either reply names a tag, a step, a mailbox, an allowance or who has access.

**2026-10-26, the record said a client had not been told, and they had.** A requester asked an Operator about two records a job could not read. Its first reply was wrong; 50 minutes later it sent a second reply correcting itself. A session checking what the requester had been told read the request cards and the `[SMD Ops]` emails, found only the first reply, and told the Captain the question was still open. A `replied` card counts the replies sent before it lands and is never sent again, so any later reply in the same request, and any reply to a message the seat has no request for, reached the client and no record SMD reads. Now every such reply posts a `followup` card quoting its own opening, emailed as `[SMD Ops] <seat> later reply sent at +<n>m` (migration 0141; `.claude/bin/requests` lists it as `later reply sent`). Replies sent before the change are not backfilled. Before telling anyone what a client has or has not been told, read the thread itself; the cards are an index, not the mailbox.

**2026-10-27, a card says what was asked and what was done.** The Captain asked that every request email clearly state what the request is, who made it, and the Operator's reply and action. A card had carried the subject line, the first 300 characters of the reply, a bare address, a raw tool list and UTC times, so the ask itself was never in it. Now the body runs in four parts. WHO ASKED gives the firm's authored name for the sender (`users[].full_name` in the seat's `customer.yaml`, never the email's display name, and the address alone when none is authored). WHAT THEY ASKED gives the subject and the person's own words in full. THE OPERATOR'S REPLY gives the reply in full. WHAT THE OPERATOR DID groups each tool it ran under what that tool can do (sent, recorded or drafted, looked up), with call counts and any job it started. Times are Arizona time. The seat keeps the whole texts (up to about 4,000 characters each) in its request index under the same seven-day custody as the openings (`shared/request_index.py`), and they still reach the email and nothing else. A card from a seat on an older image falls back to the openings and the raw tool list. Subjects are unchanged, so mail filters keep working.

**2026-10-13, a new kind of ask waits for a proof run.** About 45 of A&P's 52 defects from 09-01 to 10-13 happened in a lane's first one to three live uses, and wherever a dry run ran first nothing reached the firm. So a seat holds a queued job of any kind not proven on it (for drafting, per document class), keeps it queued (never failed), and posts the NEW KIND OF ASK card at once, naming the held job and the command that proves it. SMD runs the job's pipeline as a proof run on the request's own matter (nothing filed, nothing emailed, the job row untouched), reads what it made, and accepts it; the job then runs live on the lane's next tick. An in-turn skill not yet proven on the seat pages once on its first use (`NEW KIND OF ASK, first use`) and is never held. The proven set is `operator/customers/<slug>/lane-proofs.yaml` plus what `medchron lane-proof accept` records on the seat. Runbook: `docs/runbooks/operator/lane-proof.md`.

**2026-10-13, proven delivery.** Until then a job was `delivered` because its own runner said so. Three wrong outputs reached a firm that way with no alert (a false coverage report filed in place of a demand, a wrong coverage report emailed to the requester, a discovery declaration swearing to a count it did not have), and a dry run counted as a delivery. Every job lane now does two things before `delivered` is recorded. First, an independent review reads the work before anything is filed: code checks the content against its own sources, and a separate model with its own adversarial prompt (never the drafter's model grading itself) reads prose and legal output. Per lane: the chronology gains a review stage before upload; the demand reviews the letter and any coverage report; drafting reviews every document on the firm's `review` model (falling back to its audit model); the litigation workbook is checked by code against its own data (every in-scope matter listed once, rows that agree with the data, no system wording); the negotiation watch checks each offer row's amount and date against the document and takes a second model read, and a row that fails is not entered. Second, the far end is read back: every filed file by id, size and sha256 against the bytes built, and negotiation rows by re-reading the written fields; agent-driven Smokeball writes read back too and return `readback_mismatch` or `link_not_visible` when the far end disagrees. A proven job writes a `DELIVERY_PROVEN` audit row (file ids, sizes, hashes, the review's status, model and cost in `review_cents`). A failure writes `DELIVERY_PROOF_FAILED` with a closed code (`proof_missing`, `readback_unproven`, `readback_mismatch`, `review_missing`, `review_held`, `dry_run_reported`, `wake_lost`, `reply_unproven`, `reply_not_in_sent_items`, and for a chronology or litigation hold SMD's side caused: `gate_held`, `update_held`, `upload_held`, `stage_refused`, `limit_held`, `identity_held`). A dry run outside a proof run pages `dry_run_reported`; a proof run's dry run is its expected ending and pages nothing. The requester's reply is watched too: until the job's reply is sent and found in Sent Items, or 20 minutes after the wake, SMD is paged. A review MUST-FIX holds the job with nothing filed (the chronology records `held`; demand, drafting and litigation record `failed`, because their `held` is final and a client reply on `failed` is refused) and the reason starts `review_held:`. SMD clears it with the lane's root-only resume (`medchron_job_resume`, `demand_job_resume`, `drafting_job_resume`, `litigation_job_resume`, `negotiation_job_resume`) carrying either `release_review: true` (file the work as it stands, every finding written into the attorney notes) or `redo_review: true` (review afresh after a fix, paid once more), never both. Neither has an agent tool, so no email can talk a seat into releasing a hold.

**2026-10-13, a hold SMD's side caused is never the firm's question.** A litigation job held at its gates (SMD's own seed data) woke the Operator with counts only, the skill said "surface and ask", and the Operator emailed the firm asking whether to resubmit or pass it to SMD. The runner now classifies every chronology and litigation hold (`operator/runners/medchron/medchron/proof/holds.py`) into a closed, firm-safe hold class, and each skill maps it to fixed wording (`references/reply-shapes.md` in `litigation-status` and `medical-chronology-maintainer`). Every SMD-side class tells the firm what is on the matter now, that nothing is needed from it, and that SMD is finishing it, and pages SMD under the codes above with the technical reason, which reaches only the alert and the job row. The firm is asked something only when the answer is its own: a record in the file that names another person, two chronologies that could each be current, or (at request time) no matter or more than one. A chronology update's job reason carries counts, paragraph indexes and document ids, never record text or another person's name or date of birth (`base_proof.json` and `identity_holds.json` on the seat keep the detail). For the chronology lane this was superseded on 2026-10-24 (below): a held chronology tells the firm nothing at all.

**2026-10-24, a chronology reaches the firm only when it is delivered.** The Captain's two decisions, for the chronology lane only (demand, drafting, litigation and negotiation are unchanged). First, the firm hears from the Operator about a chronology only on delivery. A held chronology gets no DELIVER wake and no reply watch, and the broker refuses it a reply exactly as it refuses a failed job ("Send nothing to anyone"); SMD is still paged on every stop, now including a chronology that already covers the new records and an update with two candidate chronologies (both under `update_held`), which used to page nothing because the firm was told. The one held chronology that still wakes the Operator is a RUN refused on a matter that already holds a delivered chronology: the turn resubmits it as the UPDATE and sends nothing, and SMD is paged `stage_refused` if no UPDATE is queued in 20 minutes. A requester who writes in the thread of a chronology not yet delivered gets "under way" and nothing about why it stopped. Second, a record that names a person other than the client no longer holds a chronology or asks the firm anything. Wherever it is found (an emailed attachment at the fold, an attachment's transcription at the identity stage, a file no client's folder owns at `build_units`, or a new document in an update), it is withheld by rule: recorded in `identity_release.json` as `by: "rule"`, read by no stage, and named in the delivered package's Records Reviewed section with the reason "names a person other than the client, so it was left out of this chronology" (on a joint matter, a file no client's folder or name owns: "could not be placed with one client on this matter, so it was left out of this chronology"). The other person's name or date of birth never appears; what was found stays in `identity_holds.json` or `orphan_holds.json` on the seat for SMD. A withhold the firm directed (`identity_decide`, below) keeps its wording, "left out at the firm's direction".

**2026-10-25, an update that finds nothing new is delivered, not held.** An update into a delivered chronology that concludes nothing new (every named file excluded by the firm's own rules, no page left to read, or pages read that record no visit the chronology does not already list) used to hold under `update_held` and page SMD; the all-excluded case was even paged as "a named document id is not on the matter". It now ends `delivered` with outcome `already_current`: the delivered chronology stands as it is (nothing deleted), and the runner writes `update_outcome.json` naming every requested file with a coded reason (billing pages only, read in full with no visit to add, every page already in the delivered exhibits, a copy, excluded by the firm's rule with the rule's words, no record text, and so on) and the counts and pages per reason. From that record it files one document beside the chronology, the records review: one row per requested file with its name, kind, pages and what was done with it (the firm's attorney had asked what the records were, how many pages, and which were duplicates). An update that does add entries files the same review beside the updated chronology.
