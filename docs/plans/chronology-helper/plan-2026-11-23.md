## For the Captain (first screen)

**What changes.** When an A&P chronology holds, fails or stalls, the hourly-check machine notices within 10 minutes and opens a helper session on the A&P workspace. That is the same kind of session that rescued jobs overnight, now started without anyone. It finds the cause, then either resumes the job or writes a code fix. A fix is proven on the job's saved inputs at no cost and merged through the normal tests and review. When it reaches the seat, the helper resumes the job and confirms delivery.

**What reaches you, by email only:**

1. A job that spends past the notice line. It keeps running, and you can reply "stop".
2. A job whose only way forward changes what the firm is told, such as a possible other person's records.
3. A job the helper could not finish, with the date it will expire. Five jobs have died silently this way.
4. A helper fix that broke the release to the test seat. It is undone automatically, and you are told.

**The helper never:** messages the firm, decides that a page belongs to the client, files by hand, stops a job someone else is working on, or skips the test seat.

**Left out:**

- Errors found only after delivery (8 of 30 jobs). Nothing triggers on a job that looks finished.
- Stopping jobs that run long without holding. The helper reports them.
- Other job types. They come next, on the same rails.

**First step, before any building:** a blind test. I give the helper five past jobs that held, with the code as it was before their real fix, and score its fix against the one that was actually shipped. It costs nothing; the reasoning runs on the Max login. If it lands fewer than 3 of 5, I come back to you with a different design instead of building this one.

**Your two numbers (money and what the firm is billed).** Each default below is what I use unless you say otherwise.

- **Notice line per job: $450.** That is today's per-job cap. Jobs no longer stop at it; you get one email and the job runs on. A hard stop stays at **$1,500** per job as a runaway guard. That figure is the largest budget you have approved, on an update cycle (11-20).
- **Monthly budget: it stops refusing jobs.** Today a job refuses once the month passes $1,500. On 11-21 you said "let it run over and then bill them". So the month total becomes a billing line in your email instead of a wall.

**Bill.**

- The blind test, about 1.5 hours.
- 5 PRs, about 2.5 to 3 hours of merge queue, run in parallel.
- One release, to the test seat and then the A&P seat.
- One config change to A&P's limits.
- Workspace disk cleanup by the workspace itself. It has 683 MB free of 147 GB.
- A token so the hourly-check machine can wake the workspace. I mint it.

## Design (revised after critique)

**Shape.** The helper is an ordinary handed-over workspace session (Simplifier). The hard limits live in code and in deny rules (Devil's Advocate).

- **Trigger: `operator/ops-machine/bin/ops-triage`, new 10-minute no-agent path.**
  - It reuses the work-health evaluation (`operator/bin/lib/work_health.py` `job_items`). It acts only on actionable medchron items for seats in `operator/ops-machine/workspaces.json`.
  - Actionable means `job_failed`, `job_stuck`, and `job_held` except identity holds and the firm-question classes from `hold_class.classify`. Waiting states never wake the workspace (DA 7).
  - It wakes `ops-<slug>` and writes the task with `ops-handoff`'s own steps (laptop/ops-handoff lines 146-185, moved into a shared script). It uses a new workspace token on smd-ops.
  - At most one helper per job, and a dead session is restarted at most twice.
  - The hourly model triage marks a helper-owned item as HANDLED (`TRIAGE.md`).
- **Brief: `operator/ops-machine/helper/BRIEF.md`, checked in.**
  - The playbook by hold class.
  - Prove at $0 on saved inputs, with a replay that fails on main and passes on the branch, before any PR.
  - One PR per defect: search open PRs first.
  - Before queueing, check open PRs on the same files.
  - A second Opus reviewer reads any diff touching gates, proof, the review stage or the broker.
  - Label the PR `helper`, and wait for the seat's bundle.
  - Resume with redo, confirm DELIVERY_PROVEN, and write the outbox with codes only.
- **Deny rules: `operator/ops-machine/helper/settings.json`**, passed by `ops-start` to helper sessions only.
  - Denied: `deliver_by_smd`, `*_decide`, `release_review`, `jobs.stop`, `gh workflow run release-tick` with `seat=`, `git push` to main, edits under `.github/`, `.claude/`, `operator/fleet/` and `firm.yaml`, and Gmail or Graph send tools.
  - A selftest canary proves the deny rules hold before the trigger is switched on.
- **In code, not the brief:**
  1. **Identity holds:** never helper-resumable. The resume verb rejects `--by helper:*` on `identity:` (DA 4). They are mailed to you as "possible other person's records" with counts.
  2. **Upload holds:** resumable only after a code read-back of the matter folder, in `medchron_verbs.py` `_resume`. Otherwise mailed (DA 5).
  3. **Cost:** set `per_job_cap_usd` to the hard stop, and remove `monthly_budget_usd` as a refusal in `limits.py`, keeping it as a reported figure. The notice-line email is built by the trigger from ledger cents.
  4. **Mail text and expiry date:** built from templates and `retention.py` (DA 13). The same trigger sends them via Resend, once per key.
  5. **Twin failure on a `helper`-labelled PR:** a revert PR is opened automatically and mailed (DA 2). This lives in `release-tick.yml`, or in the trigger reading rollout rows.
  6. **Claim:** `~/state/helper/<job>.claim` on the workspace. `ops-handoff` prints it, and refuses a task naming a claimed job unless `--take` is passed (DA 10).
  7. **Disk:** `ops-sweep` retention for `~/work/helper/*` after delivery, plus a disk-low email (DA 9).
- **Account limits:** helper sessions run on a dedicated account letter in the `claude` shim. Interactive failover already covers sessions (DA 8).

## Examples (invented values)

1. 01M9CCCC fails on an API timeout at 2:10 AM. It is resumed by 2:20 and delivered by 3:05. No email.
2. 01M9DDDD holds on a review false alarm. The helper's PR merges at 3:34, the seat takes it after the running job ends at 4:10, and the job resumes and is delivered at 5:00. No email.
3. 01M9BBBB passes $450 at 1:00 AM. One email arrives, and the job keeps running and delivers.
4. 01M9EEEE holds on identity. One email arrives with the page counts. The helper does not touch it.
5. The same defect holds two jobs. One PR is opened, and both resume after its release.
6. A laptop session hands over a task on a claimed job. `ops-handoff` says the helper has it and refuses without `--take`.
7. The helper's PR fails on the test seat. It is reverted automatically, you get one email, and other releases continue.
8. What works today still works: a job that delivers on its own is never touched.
