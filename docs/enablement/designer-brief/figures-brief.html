Operator Figures Brief

We need three explanatory figures redrawn by an information designer. The content is finished and checked against the product. The job is craft: layout, hierarchy, rhythm and finish, so that each figure stands beside the best product diagrams in the industry.

1. The job

SMD Services builds and runs a managed AI worker for businesses, called the Operator. These three figures carry a first technical conversation between our sales staff and a buyer. They sit in a seven-figure set; the other four stay as they are for now and will be restyled by us to match your system.

  • Figure 1, Before and after. What changes for a business.
  • Figure 2, System context. What the Operator touches and who runs what.
  • Figure 4, The permission model. The client writes limits; here is a rule set beside its effects.

Audience: an experienced enterprise technical seller, and through them the owner, administrator or IT reviewer at a small or mid-sized business. Read on a shared screen and on a slide.

Fixed. Do not change.

  • Every label, word for word (section 3 lists them).
  • Every box, every arrow, its direction and its label.
  • Which boxes sit in which zone.
  • The numbers on Figure 2's arrows: they match numbered notes in the page text.
  • What each treatment means (section 4).
  • No added icons that stand for a brand, no robots, brains, shields or gears, no people illustrations.

Open. This is your craft.

  • Layout, proportion, spacing and alignment.
  • Type sizes and weights within the two families.
  • Line weights, corner treatment, arrow style.
  • How the three ownership treatments and the status colours look, as long as each meaning stays distinct without relying on colour alone.
  • A small, consistent set of generic pictograms if they add clarity (one family, one size).
  • A better composition for any figure, if every fixed item survives.

If you think a label is unclear or a relationship is wrong, tell us. Do not fix it silently: the wording is checked against the running product.

2. The standard

These are the figures ours should stand beside. Look at how plain they are: the credibility comes from named parts, clear boundaries and numbered steps, not from decoration.

  • Tailscale, How Tailscale works: small figures, one idea each; a rule shown beside its effect.
  • Cloudflare, Enterprise AI agent workspace: zones, numbered steps matched to text.
  • HashiCorp Vault, Architecture: twelve plain boxes and one labelled boundary.

What we know is weak in the current drawings

  • Figure 1 does its job but is plain. The single change (the middle slot) should be unmistakable at a glance.
  • Figure 2 is correct and complete but dense: nine numbered connections, some sharing a number. It needs calmer routing and a clearer reading order without losing a connection.
  • Figure 4 is the strongest. It needs finish, and the three outcomes (allowed, waits for a person, refused) should read instantly.

  • Type. Archivo for names and text, Archivo Narrow in small capitals for zone labels, figure titles and legends. No third family.
  • Register. Flat and plainspoken, like good commercial signage. No gradients, shadows, glows, 3D or stock illustration.
  • Every figure carries a title ("FIGURE 2 · SYSTEM CONTEXT"), a version stamp ("SMD SERVICES · V3 · 2026-11-09") and a legend for the treatments it uses.
  • Arrows have one head and a label. Flow runs one way where the content allows.
  • Legibility. Readable on a shared screen without zooming; smallest text no smaller than the current subtitles relative to the figure width.

5. What to deliver

  • An editable source file (Figma preferred; Illustrator accepted) with text as live text, built from components and named styles.
  • For each figure: SVG in light and dark with text kept as text in the named fonts, and PNG at 2x.
  • A 16:9 slide-ready variant of each figure.
  • A one-page style sheet: the components, type sizes, line weights and colour tokens you used, so the remaining four figures can be matched.

How we will accept the work

  • Every fixed label and relationship in section 3 is present, word for word.
  • Each treatment keeps its one meaning across all three figures, and no meaning depends on colour alone.
  • No text touches or crosses a line or box edge; every arrow meets a named box; nothing is clipped; both themes hold.
  • Each figure answers its one question without the page text beside it.
  • Two rounds of revisions after the first proofs.

Terms we will ask for

  • Work made for hire with full assignment of rights to SMDurgan, LLC.
  • The content is confidential until we publish it; no portfolio use without our written permission.
