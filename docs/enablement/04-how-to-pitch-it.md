## Qualifying (MEDDPICC, mapped to our deals)

| Letter                | What to find out                                                                                            | Good sign                                                   |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Metrics**           | How many matters? How many pages of records a month? How long does a chronology or demand draft take today? | Volume high enough that a coordinator seat is clearly full. |
| **Economic buyer**    | Who signs? Usually the managing partner.                                                                    | You're talking to them, or they asked for the meeting.      |
| **Decision criteria** | Confidentiality, accuracy, "will my team use it", cost compared to a hire.                                  | They raise confidentiality early. It means they're serious. |
| **Decision process**  | Do they need an ethics or carrier review?                                                                   | Known steps; we can hand over the procurement packet.       |
| **Paper process**     | Engagement agreement, data-processing terms.                                                                | No surprises at signature.                                  |
| **Identify pain**     | Ask about objectives first: where is the firm trying to go? Then what's in the way.                         | They describe a goal, not just a complaint.                 |
| **Champion**          | Often the office administrator or senior paralegal.                                                         | Someone who will forward the materials internally.          |
| **Competition**       | Doing nothing, hiring, point tools, outsourced services.                                                    | They've tried a tool and it didn't stick.                   |

Our rule on discovery: **objectives over problems.** Start with where the business is trying to go; the problems make sense only against that. Part 3 shows how this played out with our first client.

## Objections (battlecard)

| They say                                            | We answer                                                                                                                                                                        | Proof today                                                 |
| --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Another AI tool. I've seen people get burned."     | We're not a tool you run, we're a worker we run. It does paralegal and coordinator work, and every chronology entry is checked against its source page.                          | Demonstrable: cited chronologies                            |
| "How do I know my client data is safe?"             | Lead with this. Its own isolated environment on the firm's accounts. We see health and the record of actions, never the content.                                                 | Demonstrable: architecture; procurement packet in progress  |
| "Will my team actually use it?"                     | That's what the managed service answers. They email it like a colleague. We run it.                                                                                              | Demonstrable: email-in, work-back                           |
| "Who else has this?"                                | Be straight: we're early and selective. Offer a scoped start, point to whoever referred us, and show the real thing.                                                             | One firm in production; figures in part 1                   |
| "My paralegal can just use an AI assistant."        | Smart, and they probably should. That makes the person faster at their desk. It doesn't cover the seat when they're out or when they leave.                                      | Described                                                   |
| "We already use an outsourced service for demands." | Good product for what it is. The difference is custody and coverage: the work stays in your system, under your rules, and we cover more than one lane. Some firms will run both. | Described                                                   |
| "What does it cost?"                                | We price it like a hire, not a subscription, and it depends on the seat we build together.                                                                                       | Pricing comes from the Captain, never from public material. |

**Where we honestly lose:** a firm whose only pain is phones and intake, or only demand-letter volume, may be right to buy a point solution today. We win where the job is broader than one lane. Don't force the firms we're not for.

## Words to use, words to avoid

| Use                                                     | Avoid                                                   | Why                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| "a worker", "a staff member who happens to be software" | "AI employee" as an opener                              | It lowers trust and raises replacement fear.   |
| "paralegal and coordinator work"                        | "we don't do legal work"                                | The second is inaccurate.                      |
| "solution"                                              | "systems"                                               | "Systems" sounds like one more thing to learn. |
| "we work alongside you"                                 | "we audit your operations"                              | We're a peer, not an auditor.                  |
| "we start with a conversation"                          | specific durations or dollar amounts in public material | Both are scoped per engagement.                |
| the gap between people and software                     | anything that disparages their staff or their software  | Both are capable. The gap is the enemy.        |
