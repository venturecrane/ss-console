# Glossary: terms a new representative will hear

Plain definitions, grouped by topic. Each group ends with the documents it is drawn from.

## The business and the offer

**SMD Services.** A software and AI solutions firm for small businesses. Its flagship is the Operator. Work starts with an assessment.

**Assessment.** The first conversation with a business: how it actually runs, where it is trying to go, what is costing the most time, and what the right solution is. The result is a clear recommendation, which may be something the owner can run alone, a focused project, or an Operator.

**The gap.** The layer of work that falls between a business's people and its software: handoffs, follow-ups, reading nobody has time for. The Operator is pitched as the worker that fills it. Never pitch it by disparaging the client's staff or software; both are capable, the gap is the problem.

**Two front doors.** SMD sells either a consulting engagement (scoped work on a specific objective) or the Operator. When it is unclear which fits, the consulting conversation comes first.

**Competes with a hire.** The Operator's rival is the next person a business would hire, not a software product. Existing software is something it connects to.

**The guide.** SMD's own role: the team that configures the Operator for a business and stays with it as the technology changes. "A guide, not a vendor."

**Harness, guide and memory.** The three things that together make the Operator hard to copy: limits enforced in code (the harness), the people who set it up well for one business (the guide), and what it learns about that business over time (the memory). No single feature is the advantage.

## The product

**Operator.** SMD's configurable product: a managed AI worker configured for one business and run by SMD. It does recurring connective work on its own schedule and takes on what people hand it. Never call it an "AI employee".

**Capability.** Something SMD can configure the Operator to do for a client. Describe it in the present tense, as what the Operator does.

**Seat.** One running Operator for one client: its own isolated machine with its own encrypted storage. "One seat per client."

**Managed service.** SMD sets the Operator up, connects it, rehearses it, turns it on, watches it, updates it and changes it. The client gives feedback to a worker instead of taking on a second job running one.

**The five written surfaces.** What is authored for each client: skills, limits, voice, connections and memory. Anything not written down is refused.

**Skill.** A kind of work the Operator knows how to do, and what sets it off.

**Voice.** How the Operator's writing should read for this business, learned from the business's own documents and sharpened by staff corrections.

**Connection (connector).** Access to one of the client's systems, granted by the client and revocable by the client. SMD builds or adopts the connection for any system a client runs.

**Memory.** What the Operator keeps about how the business works and how each person likes to work. It belongs to the client, is kept on the client's own machine, and is exported to the client if the service ends.

**System of record.** The client's main business system, such as a law firm's case-management system. It stays the record; the Operator reads from it and writes its results back into it.

**Operator role (persona).** A distinct identity an Operator presents. A seat can carry more than one role.

**Industry pack.** A starting configuration for one kind of business, used for onboarding and selling. It is not a separate product. Each pack maps the seat the Operator fills in that industry and the line it never crosses; SMD shapes it with the client.

**Self-serve agent.** A smaller, published-price personal version of the Operator for one person, bought on the website and set up by SMD. Same safety controls, different commercial terms.

**Where a capability stands.** Rep knowledge, in three labels: "running for a client today", "built, set up per client", and "configured per engagement". A label answers a question about timing; it never means the Operator cannot do something.

## Doing the work

**Routine.** A named duty that runs on a schedule, such as a daily digest of what needs a person or a weekly chase for outstanding records.

**On call (work on request).** Between routines, anyone the client has authorized can email the Operator a task or a question and get the answer back in the same thread.

**Queued job (lane).** Work that takes many steps or real cost, such as a medical chronology or a document draft. It runs outside the chat reply, with fixed stages, a cost limit, an independent review, and proof of delivery.

**Proof before first use (proof run).** A kind of job runs on a real request only after a proof run on that client's own inputs, with nothing written to the client's system, has passed and been accepted.

**Held job.** A job that stopped on purpose before delivering, usually because a review found something to fix. Nothing was filed; a person decides what happens next.

**Delivery proven.** A job counts as delivered only when the delivered file has been fetched back from the client's system and matches what was built.

**Rehearsal.** Testing on practice data SMD owns, including deliberately broken and hostile material, before anything touches a client's account.

## Control and safety

**Limit (ceiling).** For each kind of action, how far the Operator may go: act on its own, ask first, draft for a person, or nothing written and therefore refused. The ceiling in the agreement is the highest a setting can ever go.

**Unwritten means refused (fail-closed).** If no limit is written for an action, or a safety check cannot complete, the action is refused rather than waved through.

**Routine levels.** Shown in the Client portal as "Surfaces it", "Prepares it for you" and "Handles it". In engagement documents they appear as flag-only, prepare-and-route and auto-handle.

**Draft for review.** For a message leaving the business, the Operator writes the draft and a person sends it.

**Ask first (confirm).** The Operator asks a named person yes or no in the same conversation and acts only on a yes.

**Lines that never move.** Moving money is banned outright. Agreeing to something on the client's behalf and anything it cannot undo always wait for a person. For a law firm, anything to a court or opposing counsel is always sent by a person.

**Broker.** A separate component that holds the credentials able to send, checks every recipient, and writes the audit entry. The Operator itself can never read those credentials.

**Inbound fence.** Content from outside, such as an email or attachment, is treated as information, never as instructions. Text from an unknown sender is quarantined so instructions hidden in it cannot drive an action.

**Audit record.** One entry per action, each chained to the one before it so a change shows. The Operator can read it and cannot rewrite it. Say "tamper-evident", never "tamper-proof".

**Evidence packet.** A copy of the audit record a client can hand to anyone, who can check it on SMD's public trust page without asking SMD.

**Client portal.** Where the client's administrators see what the Operator is set up to do, read the audit record, move limit settings up to the ceiling, request a change, and pause it. It never shows the client's business data.

**Pause.** One control in the Client portal that stops all activity at once until someone turns it back on.

**Named Administrator.** The client's person, named in the agreement, who may administer the Operator: change routine levels, apply firm-wide rules, approve larger acts.

Sources: How the Operator Works, views 3, 4 and 7 (file 06); product library, permission-model.md and client-controls.md (file 05); website trust page (file 04).

## Data and the service

**Isolation.** Each client's machine, storage, credentials, memory, audit record and mailbox are never shared with another client.

**Who handles client files.** Only AI agents: the client's own Operator, and SMD's agents in a workspace sealed to that client. No person at SMD reads client files.

**Model provider.** The AI company that processes task content. It processes under terms that bar training on it, and SMD gives clients notice before changing it.

**Sub-processor.** An outside service SMD relies on to run the Operator, such as the cloud host. Clients get notice before one changes.

**Heartbeat and work-health check.** SMD's monitoring: each seat reports its health continuously, and a regular check reads the work itself and flags anything stuck, held, failed or unanswered.

**Onboarding phases.** Prepare, Explore, Realize, Deploy, Run. Each ends on proof, not on a date. Billing starts only after SMD states testing is complete and the client starts the subscription.

## Law firm words you will hear in the industry example

**Medical chronology.** A dated summary of a client's medical treatment read from the medical records, each entry cited to the page it came from.

**Demand letter.** The letter a plaintiff's firm sends an insurance carrier setting out the case and asking for payment. The Operator drafts it; the attorney judges and signs.

**Case evaluation memo.** An internal memo setting out what a case is worth and why. The Operator drafts it from the records; the attorney judges it.

**Mediation brief.** The document a firm prepares for a settlement mediation.

**Matter.** One case in the firm's system.
