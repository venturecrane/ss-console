# What the Operator is: a plain overview for a new sales representative

This page is written for someone who has never seen the product. It gathers what the other files in this collection say into one short picture. Every statement comes from SMD's own documents, named at the end of each section.

## The company

SMD Services is a software and AI solutions firm for small businesses, based in Phoenix and working with businesses in the Valley and beyond. It works alongside business owners to understand where they are trying to go, figure out what is slowing them down, and build the right solution together. Solutions range from a clearer process and better use of existing tools to custom internal tools, connecting systems, operational dashboards, and AI and automation where AI is the right answer. SMD names AI as a capability; it does not brand the firm as "AI-powered", and part of its job is saying when AI is not the answer. Every relationship starts with an assessment, and if an Operator is not the fit, SMD says so.

Sources: the website home and Operator pages (file 02); the product library, what-it-is.md (file 05).

## The gap the Operator fills

Every business has people who are good at their jobs and software that is good at its job. Between them sits a layer of work nobody owns: the handoff from one system to the next, the follow-up that slips, the reading nobody has time for. Usually a person holds it together by hand, and it gets done late, done twice, or not at all. The Operator is the worker that fills that gap.

Source: website home page (file 02).

## What the Operator is

The Operator is SMD's configurable product: a managed AI worker. A business decides what it does, and SMD runs it. SMD configures one for a specific business, gives it a role, connects it to the systems the business already uses (such as its email and its system of record), and stays accountable for how it works. People reach it the way they reach a colleague, mainly by email.

It is not a chatbot. A chatbot waits for someone to type a question. The Operator has standing work it does on its own schedule, such as watching for what is slipping or sending a recurring update, and it also carries to done anything a person hands it.

It has no fixed job. For each client, five things are written down: its skills (the kinds of work it knows how to do), its limits (how far it may go for each kind of action), its voice (how its writing should read), its connections (which of the client's systems it may reach), and its memory (what it keeps about the client's work and people). Anything not written down is refused. Because the Operator is configured this way, a capability is something SMD can set up for a client, and a representative describes it in the present tense.

Sources: product library, what-it-is.md (file 05); How the Operator Works, "One worker, configured for one business" (file 06); website Operator page, "Is the Operator just a chatbot?" (file 02).

## What it is sold against

The Operator competes with a hire, not with software. Its rival is the next person a business would hire: a coordinator, an intake lead, a client-service associate, an office manager. The business's existing systems are things it connects to, not things it replaces, and the more disconnected systems a business runs, the more an Operator is worth. Price conversations compare to a salary, never to a software subscription. A representative never quotes a price; pricing is set by SMD for each engagement.

Sources: enablement kit part 1 (file 06); product library, who-smd-sells-to.md (file 05).

## What it does day to day

Two kinds of work, side by side:

- Standing work. Scheduled routines that run on their own clock: a morning digest of what needs a person, a sweep for deadlines, a chase for outstanding documents. A routine re-reads what is outstanding every time it runs, so an outage delays work rather than losing it.
- Work on request. Anyone the business has authorized emails the Operator like a colleague and gets the work back in the same thread. Work with many steps or real cost, such as reading thousands of pages of medical records into a chronology, runs as a queued job with cost limits, an independent review, and proof that the result was delivered to the right place.

The industry pages on the website show the seat the Operator fills in each kind of business, and each one is something SMD configures the Operator to do. One industry example is running for a client today: for a personal injury law firm, the Operator has delivered 18 medical chronologies from more than 29,000 pages of records, every entry cited to the page it came from, along with first drafts for the attorneys to judge and finish. Proof figures are used exactly as published on the website case study.

Sources: product library, how-the-operator-works.md, jobs.md and industry-packs.md (file 05); website case study and industry pages (files 02 and 03).

## Who controls it

- The client writes the limits. For every kind of action the Operator can take, the client sets whether it acts on its own, asks first, drafts for a person to send, or may not do it at all. Where no limit is written, the action is refused.
- In the Client portal the limits show as three plain settings per routine: "Surfaces it", "Prepares it for you", "Handles it". Each routine has a ceiling set in the agreement, and nobody, including SMD and the Operator, can raise a routine above it without changing the agreement.
- Some lines never move: the Operator does not move money, agreeing to something on the client's behalf and anything it cannot undo always wait for a person, and the Operator can never give itself more authority.
- Every action is written to an audit record the Operator can read but cannot rewrite. Say "tamper-evident", never "tamper-proof".
- The client can pause it from the Client portal at any time, or cut its access in the client's own systems.

Sources: How the Operator Works, views 4 and 7 (file 06); product library, permission-model.md and client-controls.md (file 05); website Operator page (file 02).

## Where the data lives

Each client gets its own isolated machine with its own encrypted storage. Nothing is shared with another client, and what one client's Operator learns never benefits another client. The client's files stay in the client's own systems; SMD does not warehouse copies. No person at SMD reads client files: they are handled only by AI agents, the client's own Operator and SMD's agents working in a workspace sealed to that client. The Operator's mailbox lives in the client's own mail system. Task content sent to the AI model provider is processed under terms that bar training on it. What the Operator learns about the business belongs to the business and is exported to the client if the service ends.

Sources: How the Operator Works, views 2, 6 and 7 (file 06); product library, isolation-and-data-custody.md (file 05); website security page (file 04).

## Why "managed" matters

The client is not buying software to run. SMD sets it up with the client, wires it into the client's tools, rehearses it on practice data that includes hostile test cases, turns it on one area at a time, watches its health and its work through the day, and releases improvements to it. Billing starts only after SMD states in writing that testing is complete and the client starts the subscription.

Sources: product library, managed-service.md (file 05); How the Operator Works, "What managed means" (file 06).

## Where each capability stands, for the representative

A representative knows where each capability stands for clients today, because a prospect will ask "who runs this today?" and "what would you set up for us?". The product library uses three labels:

- **Running for a client today**: for example one isolated machine per client, limits enforced at every action, drafts for a person and sends to listed people, the audit record with daily verification, email through a mailbox in the client's own system, queued jobs with review and proof of delivery, pause in the Client portal, SMD's monitoring, and the law firm industry pack.
- **Built, set up per client**: for example chat and voice-note transcription, the AI assistant connector, more than one Operator role on a seat, and the client level dial in the Client portal.
- **Configured per engagement**: for example texting, team chat, a connection to any other system a client runs, and the industry-specific skills of each industry pack beyond the law firm, which SMD builds with the first client in that industry.

Every one of these is something the Operator does once SMD configures it. A label answers a question about timing; it is never a reason to say the Operator cannot do something. The full list is built-versus-planned.md in file 05.

## How to talk about it

- Lead with objectives, not problems: start with where the business is trying to go.
- Be a peer, not an auditor: "we work alongside you".
- Say "solution", not "systems", in positioning talk.
- No dollar amounts and no fixed timeframes, ever.
- Never claim to know a prospect's business before the conversation.
- Speak as the firm: "we" and "our team".
- Never promise a fit, a price, a start date or a result. When unsure, bring the prospect to SMD at team@smd.services.
