How the Operator Works

The Operator is a managed worker that SMD builds for one business and runs for it. It does the connecting work between that business's people and its systems, inside limits the business writes down, and it keeps an audit record of every action. This page explains the product in seven views, each with one figure, from the idea to the mechanism.

One worker, configured for one business

The Operator has no fixed job. Five things are written down for each client, and anything not written down is refused.

SkillsThe kinds of work it knows how to do, and what sets each one off.
LimitsHow far it may go for each kind of action, set by the client.
VoiceHow the business writes, by type of document.

It is sold against the next hire, a coordinator or an assistant, and never against software. The client's existing systems are things it connects to.

View 2 · system context

What it touches, and who runs what

The client's systems stay the systems of record. Each client gets its own machine. SMD runs the service from outside it.

{{FIG2}}
  1. 1Staff ask by email. A request is an email to the Operator's own mailbox, which sits in the client's mail tenant. Only people on the client's staff list are answered.
  2. 2Mail is pulled in and verified. The seat pulls new mail from that mailbox, and the front door verifies every inbound message before any work starts.
  3. 3It works in the client's systems. It reads what the task needs and writes results back. SMD does not warehouse copies of the client's files. Work SMD staff must look at stays in that client's own ops workspace and is deleted after 30 days.
  4. 4Sending is held apart. The agent cannot send on its own keys. A separate broker holds the send credentials, checks every recipient, and writes each action to the audit record.
  5. 5The portal directs, it does not hold data. Administrators see what the Operator is set up to do and the audit record of what it did, move limit settings up to the agreed ceiling, and pause it.
  6. 6The seat reports out. A health signal every minute, the newest audit entry's fingerprint, and a notice for each staff request.
  7. 7Changes come in one way. Configuration is written by SMD as a reviewed change and published to the seat. The agent cannot edit its own limits.
  8. 8SMD's access is fenced too. When a person at SMD must look at a client's work, it is from a workspace that reaches that one seat, and the access is logged.
  9. 9The model provider processes, and does not keep. Task content is sent for processing under terms that bar training on it. Changing the provider takes 30 days' notice to the client. Some seats also use a speech-to-text and a web-search service.
View 3 · one request, start to finish

The life of a request

Every request passes the same checkpoints, top to bottom. Each one names what happens when it fails. This is condensed from 15 control points enforced in code.

{{FIG3}}
  1. 1Nothing unsigned gets in. Email arrives through the Operator's mailbox; system events and schedules arrive by their own doors. A bad or stale signature is rejected before any work.
  2. 2Outside content is fenced. Text from an unlisted sender is quarantined so that instructions hidden in it cannot drive actions. A turn that read untrusted content cannot send, commit or delete.
  3. 3Skills guide, the checks enforce. A skill tells the agent how to do a kind of work. It is not the control. The control is at every tool call, in steps 4 to 6.
  4. 4Every tool is classified once. Each of the 299 tools has one fixed kind of action. A tool that is not in the registry never runs.
  5. 5Unwritten means refused. The kind of action is checked against the limit the client wrote for it. Where no limit is written, the call is refused.
  6. 6Content is checked before it leaves. A number or date that does not trace to something the agent read blocks the call. Money or contract content going outside the client's staff becomes a draft for a person.
  7. 7The action and its audit entry go together. The tool runs in the client's system and the broker writes one audit entry for it. A check that cannot run blocks the call. Long or costly work is not done here: it is queued as a job, as in View 5.
  8. 8Approvals cannot be faked by the agent. A yes counts only from an administrator, or from the named person whose approval is needed, through their own channel.
  9. 9Silence is a failure. If a listed person's request has no sent reply, no queued job and no held reply after 30 minutes, SMD is paged. The hourly triage catches it again.
View 4 · the permission model

The client writes the limits. Unwritten means no.

Every action the Operator can take belongs to a kind, and the client sets a limit for each kind. One rule set, six attempts, six outcomes.

{{FIG4}}

ReadingAlways allowed inside the systems the client connected. It is the only thing that needs no grant.
  • Who writes the rule: the client decides, SMD records it as a reviewed change. Where it is enforced: on the client's seat, at every tool call. The default: refused.
  • Sends are split by recipient: the client's own staff, the client's customers, its vendors, and everyone else. Each has its own limit.
  • Some things are banned outright, whatever the client writes: moving money, and sending from a principal's own mailbox. Sending in a staff member's name happens only when that person approves the exact draft.
  • The portal shows each limit as one of three plain settings per routine: "Surfaces it," "Prepares it for you," "Handles it." Each routine has a starting setting and a ceiling taken from the agreement.
  • Ceilings hold. An administrator can raise a routine up to its ceiling. Nobody, including SMD and the Operator, can go above it without changing the agreement.
  • Said accurately: "Surfaces it" rests on the skill's instructions and is checked in the audit record. The hard enforcement is at each tool call, as in View 3.

Big jobs run as queued work, with proof at both ends

Work that takes many steps or real cost does not run in a chat reply. It runs as a queued job with fixed stages written in code.

View 6 · isolation

One seat per client, and what is shared

Is one client's data mixed with another's? No. Each client's seat is its own machine with its own storage volume. What SMD shares across clients is the service around the seats.

{{FIG6}}
  • Never shared: the machine, its storage volume, connection credentials, memory, the audit record, and the Operator's mailbox (which lives in the client's own mail tenant).
  • Shared, and kept apart by client: the Client portal, SMD's console, the pager and hourly triage, releases and configuration, and the daily audit archive.
  • What one client's Operator learns never benefits another client. That is a term of the service agreement and a property of the layout.
View 7 · data and audit record

Where the data lives, and what the audit record is

These are the statements our service agreement makes. They are worded to be exactly true.

{{FIG7}}
  1. 1The agent reads, the broker writes. The agent can read the audit record and cannot rewrite it. Only the broker writes it.
  2. 2The client can see it. An administrator views the audit record in the Client portal.
  3. 3A fingerprint leaves every minute. The fingerprint of the newest audit entry is sent to SMD's console and kept there.
  4. 4The whole record is checked daily. A verifier pulls each seat's audit record once a day.
  5. 5The check has an outside anchor. The last fingerprint held in SMD's console must be present in the record that was pulled. A break raises an alarm at SMD.
  6. 6A dated copy is kept. The verifier writes each day's copy to an archive that refuses overwrites.
  7. 7SMD reads a notice per request. Each notice carries who asked, the subject, their words, the Operator's reply and what it ran. Never a document.

The audit record

  • One entry per action.
  • Each entry is chained to the one before it, so a change shows.
  • The agent can read it and cannot rewrite it.
  • It holds what was done, by which skill, under which limit, and the outcome. It does not hold the text of the client's documents.
  • Say "tamper-evident." Do not say "tamper-proof."

What SMD can see

  • Health, settings, and the audit record of actions.
  • A notice per staff request: who asked, the subject, their words, the reply and what it ran. Never a document.
  • Client work itself only from a per-client ops workspace, with access logged.

What never happens

  • The client's files are not copied into SMD's systems.
  • Send credentials never reach the agent.
  • Client content is not used to train models.
  • The Operator does not send from a principal's own mailbox.

Standard terms

  • Security incident notice within 24 hours.
  • On exit: the audit record and memory are exported within 14 days and everything is destroyed within 30, confirmed in writing.
  • 30 days' notice before a sub-processor changes.
  • No uptime percentage and no service credits. SMD holds no SOC 2 or ISO 27001 of its own, and says so.

Proven, built, planned

A technical buyer will test these. Present each thing as what it is.

In production with a paying client

  • One isolated machine per client
  • Limits enforced at every tool call; unwritten means refused
  • Drafts for a person, and sends to listed staff, customers and vendors
  • Chained audit record with daily verification
  • Email through a mailbox in the client's own tenant
  • Queued jobs with proof gate, review and read-back
  • Pause in the portal
  • Pager, hourly work check, request notices

  • "Ask first" approvals on sends
  • The level dial in the portal
  • Client-run connections, team access and compliance view
  • Chat and voice-note channels
  • The connector that lets staff reach it from their AI assistant

  • More than one persona per seat

Planned, not built

  • Signed audit entries

Industry packs are starting configurations for onboarding. The running product is the same for every business: nothing in it branches on industry.

One example

The same model, in one industry

The first Operator runs for a personal-injury law firm. The system of record is the firm's practice-management system. Staff email requests, and the queued jobs produce medical chronologies and first drafts for an attorney to judge. So far: 18 chronologies from 29,000+ pages of records, every entry cited to the page it came from. Communications to a court or to opposing counsel are always prepared for a person to send, and nothing touching deadlines or money runs on its own. Swap the system of record, the skills and the limits, and the seven views above describe any other business.
