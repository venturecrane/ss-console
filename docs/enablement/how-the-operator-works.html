How the Operator Works

The Operator is a managed worker that SMD builds for one business and runs for it. It does the connecting work between that business's people and its systems, inside limits the business writes down, and it keeps a record of every action. This page explains the product in seven views, from the idea to the mechanism.

The Operator has no fixed job. Five things are written down for each client, and anything not written down is refused.

It is sold against the next hire, a coordinator or an assistant, and never against software. The client's existing systems are things it connects to.

View 2 · system context

What it touches, and who runs what

The client's systems stay the systems of record. Each client gets its own machine. SMD runs the service from outside it.

  1. 1Staff ask by email. Requests arrive in the Operator's own mailbox. Only people on the client's list are answered.
  2. 2It works in the client's systems. It reads what the task needs and writes results back. SMD keeps no copy of the client's files.
  3. 3Sending is held apart. The agent cannot send on its own keys. A separate broker holds the send credentials, checks every recipient, and writes each action to the record.
  4. 4The portal directs, it does not hold data. Administrators see what the Operator is set up to do and the record of what it did, move levels up to the agreed ceiling, and pause it.
  5. 5The seat reports out. A health signal every minute, the newest audit entry's fingerprint, and a notice for each staff request.
  6. 6Changes come in one way. Configuration is written by SMD as a reviewed change and published to the seat. The agent cannot edit its own limits.
  7. 7SMD's access is fenced too. When a person at SMD must look at a client's work, it is from a workspace that reaches that one seat, and the access is logged.
  8. 8The model provider processes, and does not keep. Task content is sent for processing under terms that bar training on it. Changing the provider takes 30 days' notice to the client.
View 3 · one request, start to finish

The life of a request

Every request passes the same checkpoints, top to bottom. Each one names what happens when it fails. This is condensed from 15 control points enforced in code.

  1. 2Outside content is fenced. Text from an unlisted sender is quarantined so that instructions hidden in it cannot drive actions. A turn that read untrusted content cannot send, commit or delete.
  2. 3Skills guide, the checks enforce. A skill tells the agent how to do a kind of work. It is not the control. The control is at every tool call, in steps 4 to 6.
  3. 7Approvals cannot be faked by the agent. A yes counts only from an administrator, or from the named person whose approval is needed, through their own channel.
  4. 8Silence is a failure. If a listed person's request has no sent reply, no queued job and no held reply after 30 minutes, SMD is alarmed. An hourly check catches it again.
View 4 · the permission model

The client writes the limits. Unwritten means no.

Every action the Operator can take belongs to a kind, and the client sets a limit for each kind. One rule set, five attempts, five outcomes.

Every kind of action, by limit

Kind of actionActs on its ownAsks firstDrafts for a personNothing written
Sends (four kinds, by who receives)SendsHeld until approved in the conversationBecomes a draft a person sendsRefused
Internal writesRunsNot offeredRunsRefused
CommitmentsStill needs a yes in the momentHeld for an administratorBlockedRefused
Destructive actionsStill needs a yes in the momentOne narrow case onlyRefusedRefused
ReadingAlways allowed inside the systems the client connected. It is the only thing that needs no grant.
  • Who writes the rule: the client decides, SMD records it as a reviewed change. Where it is enforced: on the client's seat, at every tool call. The default: refused.
  • Sends are split by recipient: the client's own staff, the client's customers, its vendors, and everyone else. Each has its own limit.
  • Some things are banned outright, whatever the client writes: moving money, and sending from a principal's own mailbox. Sending in a staff member's name happens only when that person approves the exact draft.
  • The client sees three plain levels per routine: "Surfaces it," "Prepares it for you," "Handles it." Each routine has a starting level and a ceiling taken from the agreement.
  • Ceilings hold. An administrator can raise a routine up to its ceiling. Nobody, including SMD and the Operator, can go above it without changing the agreement.
  • Said accurately: "Surfaces it" rests on the skill's instructions and is checked in the audit record. The hard enforcement is at each tool call, as in View 3.
View 5 · long work

Big jobs run as queued work, with proof at both ends

Work that takes many steps or real cost does not run in a chat reply. It runs as a job with fixed stages written in code.

  • Proof before first use. A kind of work never done for this client does not run on a real request. It waits while SMD runs it on that client's own data, stopped before anything is written, and a person accepts the result.
  • Delivery is proven, not assumed. A job counts as delivered only when the filed file has been fetched from the client's system and matches what was built.
  • Cost has a ceiling. A per-job limit and a monthly limit are checked before every paid step. Over the limit, the job holds and SMD is told.
  • If the reply does not go out, SMD is paged after 20 minutes.
View 6 · isolation

One seat per client, and what is shared

Is one client's data mixed with another's? No. Each client's seat is its own machine with its own storage. What SMD shares across clients is the service around the seats.

  • Never shared: the machine, its storage volume, connection credentials, working memory, the audit record, and the Operator's mailbox (which lives in the client's own mail tenant).
  • Shared, and kept apart by client: SMD's console, the pager, the release pipeline, the configuration store and the daily audit archive.
  • What one client's Operator learns never benefits another client. That is a term of the service agreement and a property of the layout.
View 7 · data and record

Where the data lives, and what the record is

These are the statements our service agreement makes. They are worded to be exactly true.

Kind of dataWhere it lives
The client's business filesIn the client's own systems. Read through granted connections while a task runs. SMD does not warehouse copies.
The Operator's emailIn the client's own mail tenant, under the client's retention and controls.
Working memoryOn the client's dedicated machine. Owned by the client. Exported on exit.
Audit recordOn the client's dedicated machine, viewable in the portal. A fingerprint of its newest entry leaves the machine every minute and is checked daily.
Connection credentialsOn the client's machine only. The ones that can send are held by the broker, where the agent cannot read them.
Settings, staff names, billingSMD's console and its listed sub-processors.
Task content sent for AI processingProcessed by the model provider for the task and not used to train models.

The audit record

  • One entry per action.
  • Each entry is chained to the one before it, so a change shows.
  • The agent can read it and cannot rewrite it.
  • It holds what was done, by which skill, under which limit, and the outcome. It does not hold the text of the client's documents.
  • Say "tamper-evident." Do not say "tamper-proof."

What SMD can see

  • Health, settings, and the record of actions.
  • A notice per staff request: who asked, the subject, and the opening of the reply.
  • Client work itself only from a per-client workspace, with access logged.

What never happens

  • The client's files are not copied into SMD's systems.
  • Send credentials never reach the agent.
  • Client content is not used to train models.
  • The Operator does not send from a principal's own mailbox.

Standard terms

  • Security incident notice within 24 hours.
  • On exit: the record and memory are exported within 14 days and everything is destroyed within 30, confirmed in writing.
  • 30 days' notice before a sub-processor changes.
  • No uptime percentage and no service credits. SMD holds no SOC 2 or ISO 27001 of its own, and says so.

One example

The same model, in one industry

The first Operator runs for a personal-injury law firm. The system of record is the firm's practice-management system. Staff email requests, and the queued jobs produce medical chronologies and first drafts for an attorney to judge. So far: 18 chronologies from 29,000+ pages of records, every entry cited to the page it came from. Communications to a court or to opposing counsel are always prepared for a person to send, and nothing touching deadlines or money runs on its own. Swap the system of record, the skills and the limits, and the seven views above describe any other business.
