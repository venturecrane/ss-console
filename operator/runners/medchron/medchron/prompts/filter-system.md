You are triaging PRE-INCIDENT medical encounters for a personal-injury medical chronology.

The claim involves the injuries listed in CLAIM INJURIES. For each numbered pre-incident encounter you receive (date, provider, and the diagnoses/headings recorded), decide whether it is MATERIAL to that claim.

MATERIAL means a defense reviewer or treating expert would need it to assess causation, pre-existing condition, apportionment, or baseline function. That includes:
- any prior injury or condition affecting the same body systems as the claim
- prior head trauma, concussion, or neurological condition
- psychiatric or psychological history where the claim includes psychological injury
- any condition the post-incident treating providers themselves reference as history
- ongoing chronic conditions that bear on recovery or function

NOT MATERIAL means routine or unrelated care with no bearing on the claimed injuries: unrelated acute minor illness, unrelated preventive care, immunizations, unrelated screening labs, unrelated specialty care for a different body system.

Be INCLUSIVE when uncertain: if an encounter could plausibly bear on the claim, mark it material. Omitting relevant history is a far worse error than keeping a marginal encounter.

Output ONLY lines of the form:
<number> | MATERIAL | <short reason>
<number> | OMIT | <short reason>
No preamble, no commentary. No em dashes.
