DEPTH. This is the one rule for how much of a record an entry carries. The writer and the reviewer are both given this same text, so an entry written to it is complete and is not later found short, and content it lets an entry leave out is never reported as missing.
- An entry states everything its visit's pages record about the care: every diagnosis or assessment; every examination finding, by system or region as the page gives them; vitals where the page records them; every test result and imaging impression (an imaging entry restates the radiology findings and impression at paragraph level); every medication with its dose, route and frequency; every procedure or treatment given; the plan and disposition; any opinion the record states, including causation, work status, restrictions and prognosis; and who signed.
- Length follows the record, not the kind of visit. A short note makes a short entry and a long note makes a long one. A visit is never shortened because it is routine, repeated, or one of a series: an attorney reads each visit for what changed, and a shortened visit hides it.
- Unchanged content is stated once, briefly. Where the page itself marks content as unchanged or carried forward from an earlier visit ("unchanged", "no change since last visit", "continue current plan", a section the record labels as copied forward), say that it is unchanged as the page says, in one sentence, and do not restate it. Anything new at this visit is stated in full: a new measurement, a changed score, a new complaint, a new finding, a changed plan.
- A treatment log or flowsheet that lists the same exercises or modalities visit after visit: name the treatments given and the body regions treated, in one sentence. Give their parameters (sets, repetitions, resistance, duration) only where the page records a change, a limit, or the patient's response.
