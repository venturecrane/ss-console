# Discovery Practice

How SMD opens a conversation with a prospect, for the discovery lesson. Product facts come only from the product library; this page is about the conversation, and it names no industry, business or person.

## Objectives before problems

Start with where the business is trying to go. A problem only makes sense against that objective. Many owners can name what hurts long before they can name what they want, and helping them say the objective out loud is part of the work. Ask about the destination first, then about what is in the way.

## Questions that open a conversation

Use your own words. These are shapes, not a script.

1. Where are you trying to take the business over the next stretch?
2. What would be different for you, personally, if that went well?
3. What gets in the way of that today?
4. When someone on your team is out, what slips?
5. Which parts of the day depend on one person remembering to do something?
6. Who else would have a say in a change like this?

Then listen. The best follow-up is usually a short version of what they just said, asked back as a question.

## Listen for the turn

The first thing a prospect asks for is often the visible symptom. Somewhere in the conversation they reframe it, in a sentence such as "really, what I need is". That sentence is the objective. Note it in their own words and come back to it.

## Show something concrete

People who cannot name their objective in the abstract will name it the moment they correct a draft. Offering to map one piece of their work back to them, and asking where it is wrong, gets a better answer than another question. A representative does not do that mapping; SMD does. Your part is to find the objective and bring the prospect to SMD.

## Words that land

- Say "we work alongside you", and "let's figure out what needs to change together".
- Say "solution" for what the business gets, and keep "system" for a specific tool they already use.
- Describe the Operator as a worker configured for one business and run by SMD, never as a tool or a software subscription.
- Leave out prices, amounts and durations. All of them are set with each business.
- Never claim to know what their business needs before they have told you.
- Never say the work replaces their people. Their people direct it and judge it.

## Qualifying while you listen

While the prospect talks, hold the signs from the product library's page on who SMD sells to: whether you are talking to the person who decides, whether someone inside would own a solution, and whether the basics are in place. Ask about these kindly. Never ask what the business earns.
