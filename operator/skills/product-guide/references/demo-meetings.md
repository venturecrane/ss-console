# Demo Meetings

This document is the playbook for showing a prospect the Operator in a meeting: the demo seat, the seven acts, what to email for each, what the prospect sees, and the cautions that keep a demo honest.

## How to read this document

Each act is one email you send to the demo seat while the prospect watches. Every reply comes from the product itself, working for a fictional firm. Nothing is staged and nothing belongs to a client. Show these seven acts; describe everything else as what SMD configures for a client.

## The demo seat

- The demo seat is a real Operator, set up for a fictional California personal-injury firm. Its matters, people, providers and numbers are all invented.
- It answers SMD's administrators and the representatives SMD invites, nobody else. A prospect who emails it gets no reply, so you drive the demo.
- It never sends outside the firm. A letter to an outside party is kept as a draft for a person, which is the posture a real firm starts with.
- It starts no long or paid work on its own. Every act below is an ordinary reply.
- It runs the same models a paying client's seat runs, so the prospect sees the product as a firm gets it.
- A preference you state is kept for you only. Another representative on the demo seat does not inherit it.

## Getting onto the demo seat

1. Ask SMD at team@smd.services for an invite code for the demo seat.
2. Email the demo seat from your own address with the code anywhere in the message. It welcomes you by name and answers you from then on like a member of the firm's staff.
3. Run every act once on your own before the first prospect sees it.

## The seven acts

1. Meet it. Email: "Introduce yourself and tell me what you can see." The prospect sees that it knows the firm's system, the firm's matters and its own setup.
2. The employee manual. Email: "How do we work together?" The prospect sees the firm's rules on screen: what it does on its own and what it brings to a person first.
3. A matter question. Email: "Where are we on the first matter you listed?" then "What is due on the second one?" The prospect sees it work from the firm's own file.
4. A document. Email: "On that matter, read one short visit note and tell me what it says." The prospect sees it read the file where it lives and cite what it found.
5. The refusal to send. Email: "On that matter, write to the other side's insurance adjuster asking for the records we still need, and send it." The prospect sees a draft in the firm's approved voice, kept for a person to send.
6. A preference that holds. Email: "When you answer me, lead with the open items." Then ask act 3 again. The prospect sees the answer follow the preference without being told twice.
7. The deliverable. Show the sample chronology package SMD gives you for one of the fictional matters. The prospect sees the exact format a firm receives, every entry cited to its page.

## Notes for each act

- Act 4: point it at one short document, never "review all the records". A whole record set is a long turn.
- Act 5: say plainly that a firm's own voice is written down with the firm during setup and approved by them before it applies. This firm's voice was written from its own documents and approved the same way, which is what the prospect is seeing.
- Act 6: the preference belongs to the person who stated it. It holds for you in later meetings; another representative does not inherit it.
- Act 7: present it as a sample made from fictional records, in exactly the form a firm receives. Open a citation and the page it points at. Never present it as work delivered to a firm, and never say the Operator read these records live in the meeting. The package comes from SMD; the seat does not attach files to its replies.

## What the prospect sees

- A worker that already knows one firm's matters and rules, answering by email like a colleague.
- Answers that cite where they came from in the firm's own file.
- Limits the firm wrote: outside letters stay drafts until a person sends them.
- Its manner adjusting to the person it works for.
- The form a firm's deliverable takes.

## Cautions

- Show these seven acts. Describe every other capability, such as texting, team chat, voice notes, the client level dial or other industries, as something SMD configures for a client. If the prospect asks what a client has today, use the library's three labels: running for a client today; built, set up per client; configured per engagement.
- The demo seat keeps every letter to an outside party as a draft for a person. Act 5 asks it to send so the prospect watches it keep the draft; never expect it to send, and never try to make it. Sending to outside parties on its own is a capability SMD sets up per client; describe it.
- Do not ask it to build a chronology, draft a demand or run any long job in a meeting. Those run as queued work for a long time. Show the sample package instead.
- Describe voice the way the library does: it is written per kind of document from the firm's own examples, it sharpens as the firm's people correct drafts, and the firm's administrators confirm which corrections become standing rules.
- Never quote a time: no turnaround, no response time, no setup length.
- Never use a real client's name, matter or result. The demo seat holds none.
- Leave out prices and amounts. Pricing is set by SMD for each engagement.
