# Who SMD Sells To

This page helps a representative decide whether a prospect is worth bringing to SMD. It is drawn from SMD's own qualification rules. Fit is settled in conversation with the owner, never decided from outside by a guess about the business.

## How to read this document

Each section below is a sign to listen for, not a test to run on someone. A prospect who shows the good signs is worth an introduction. A prospect who hits a hard stop is not a fit today, and saying so plainly is part of the job. The yellow flags are questions for the conversation, not reasons to walk away.

Nothing here is about price. Pricing is set by SMD for each engagement and is never part of a fit decision a representative makes.

## The buyer

- The buyer is the owner or managing principal: the person who signs and who decides without a committee.
- Sometimes an office manager or an operations lead starts the conversation. The owner still decides, and the conversation should reach them.
- The business is established and owner-led, with real operational load: too much for one person to hold together, and not yet large enough for a full-time operations executive.
- A business that has been running for three years or more is a good sign.

## Two front doors

SMD sells in two ways, and a prospect can come in through either one.

1. A consulting engagement. SMD works alongside the owner to understand where the business is trying to go, figures out what is in the way, and builds the right solution together. Solutions range from a clearer process to tool setup, connecting the tools the business already has, a custom internal tool, a view of how the business is running, or AI and automation where AI is the right answer. Each engagement is scoped for that business.
2. The Operator. For a prospect who already knows they want a worker that takes on coordinator work, built for one business and run by SMD as a managed service. The product library covers what it is and how it works.

When it is not clear which door fits, the consulting conversation comes first. It is where the business's objectives come out.

## Signs of a good fit

- The owner knows something is not working, will invest to change it, and makes the decision without a committee.
- They talk about where the business is trying to go, not only about what is going wrong.
- They say things owners say when the business has outgrown how it runs: "I can't step away", "customers slip through the cracks", "everything runs on spreadsheets", "our tools don't talk to each other", "we've stalled".
- Someone inside the business will own the solution after it is delivered, often an office manager or a senior staff member.
- The basics are in place: email, internet access and the software the business already uses.

## Not a fit today

These are hard stops. A prospect who hits one is not brought to SMD as an opportunity.

1. The conversation is not with the owner or another person who makes the decision.
2. The work asked for is much larger than one engagement. SMD can take it in parts, as separate engagements, but not as one.
3. There is no technology baseline at all: no email, no internet access, no existing tools.
4. The business is in crisis: active layoffs or a pending closure.

## Yellow flags to probe

These are not disqualifying on their own. Each one is a question to ask kindly in the conversation.

1. Nobody inside the business would own the solution after it is delivered.
2. The books are far behind. That can mean starting with a different problem first.
3. The owner wants a diagnosis but shows no willingness to change anything.
4. More than three people have to agree before anything is decided.

Do not ask a prospect what the business earns. The signs that a business can invest are simpler: it has been running for several years, it meets its payroll steadily, and it is not in crisis.

## Kinds of business and where conversations start

- SMD's first conversations have been with home services businesses, professional services firms, and contractors and trades. That is where to start, not a fence: any business with the good signs above is eligible, whatever its industry.
- Introductions often come through peer groups for business owners, chambers of commerce and local networking groups, accountants and bookkeepers, fractional finance leaders, commercial insurance agents, and small-business advisers.

## When you cannot tell

Fit is SMD's to confirm, in conversation with the owner. When a prospect looks close but you cannot tell, bring them to SMD at team@smd.services. Never promise a prospect a fit, a price, a start date or a result.
