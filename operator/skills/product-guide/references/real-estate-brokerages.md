# Real Estate Brokerages

This document describes how a residential real estate brokerage works, where its coordinators' time goes, what the Operator can take on for the brokerage, its agents and their clients, the line it never crosses, the systems it connects to, and questions a brokerage can explore. The industry facts come from the public sources listed at the end, with Arizona as the worked example where a state's rules matter. Every item under "What the Operator takes on" and "Systems it connects to" names the part of the library or of SMD's site it comes from. A brokerage seat is shaped with the brokerage the same way as every other industry pack: what follows is a starting point to react to, not the only way it can run.

## How the business works

- **The broker supervises.** In Arizona the employing broker and the designated broker must exercise reasonable supervision and control over the brokerage's licensees, keep written policies and procedures, and review the brokerage's transactions. Electronic review of a document is accepted in place of initials when the review is recorded in a history that cannot be altered.
- **Agents sell, mostly as independent contractors affiliated with a broker.** The typical member of the national association closed 10 transactions in 2024. Agents also move between brokerages: in a 2025 study of productive agents across four large MLS regions, annual turnover was 6.8 percent, with moves to a different brand reported at 5.5 percent.
- **Teams, assistants and transaction coordinators carry the paperwork.** Arizona's regulator publishes requirements for teams and an advisory list of what an unlicensed assistant may do. It bars an unlicensed assistant from originating documents, giving a consumer advice or a price or value opinion, and negotiating. Transaction coordinators are the administrative hub from contract to close: documents, deadlines, the brokerage's file review, and messages among the parties. Duties differ from office to office.
- **The listing side.** A listing agreement is signed and the listing goes into the multiple listing service (the MLS) under its rules. The seller prepares disclosures, the home is marketed and shown, open houses are held, and offers come in. Rejected offers are kept in the file.
- **The buyer side.** Since the 2024 settlement involving the national association, an MLS participant working with a buyer needs a written buyer agreement before touring a home, and it must state compensation in a way that is objectively ascertainable. Then comes the offer, earnest money, escrow, the seller's disclosure statement, the title commitment, the inspection period with the buyer's inspection notice and the seller's response, the loan and appraisal, the walkthrough, and the close of escrow when the deed is recorded.
- **Files are records.** Arizona requires transaction and employment records to be kept for years, at the broker's office or a storage location the regulator has been told about. The transaction file holds items such as earnest money receipts, closing statements and contracts.
- **Responsiveness matters to buyers.** In a 2014 survey, buyers named responsiveness the trait they most wanted in an agent.

## Where coordinator time goes

- **The contract-to-close file.** Every step of a purchase produces a document, a signature, a date or a status message, to the parties: buyer, seller, the other agent, escrow, the lender and the inspector. The broker's file review then needs the finished file.
- **Dates inside the contract.** The disclosure delivery, the title review, the inspection period and the inspection notice each carry a date that the contract sets, and the contract sets what follows when one is missed.
- **The buyer agreement.** Every buyer file must hold a signed agreement before the first showing, which adds a document and a compensation conversation at the start of each relationship.
- **Open houses.** Every visitor is asked to sign in, under the national association's safety guidance, and then the sign-ins need sorting and follow-up.
- **Consumer follow-up.** Leads and past clients depend on timely, consistent follow-up, and brokerages hold that follow-up to the same standard for every person.
- **Status questions.** Buyers and sellers ask where things stand, and agents ask the coordinator the same thing.
- **Agent support.** Recruiting, onboarding and keeping new agents productive.

## What the Operator takes on

For the brokerage:

- Open-house visitor capture, the starting preset for a brokerage seat. (From [industry-packs.md](industry-packs.md), "Real estate".)
- Keeping each transaction moving: it watches the work in flight, spots what did not happen, and nudges the right person or drafts the message. (From SMD's Operator page, "Where It Fits", the "Keeping work moving" lens.)
- Work too long or too costly for a reply runs as a queued job, with fixed stages, cost limits, an independent review, and proof that the result landed in the brokerage's own system. A new kind of queued job is set up per engagement on the same stages. (From [jobs.md](jobs.md), "Job versus chat turn", "Kinds of job" and "From request to delivery".)
- A record of every action it takes, where the brokerage can see it. (From [industry-packs.md](industry-packs.md), "The rule in every industry".)

For its agents and staff:

- Intake and first response for new inquiries: it reads the request, pulls the details that matter, drafts the response in the agent's voice, and books the next step. (From SMD's Operator page, "Where It Fits", the "Intake and first response" lens.)
- Drafts sent in an agent's own name only on that agent's approval: the Operator proposes, the agent approves by replying, and the message goes from the agent's address. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Email channels".)
- One Operator for the whole team, so what it learns from everyone's corrections stays with the brokerage when an agent moves on. (From SMD's Operator page, "Yours To Keep".)

For the brokerage's clients:

- Recurring communication such as status updates and scheduling. For each kind of message, the brokerage chooses whether the draft goes to an agent to review or goes out on its own. (From SMD's Operator page, "Where It Fits", the "Recurring client communication" lens.)
- Reaching clients on the channels the brokerage chooses, such as email, and texting where the brokerage wants it, which SMD sets up, through the same gate and rules. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Email channels" and "Other channels".)

## The line it never crosses

- **It drafts for the agent.** The Operator never contacts a consumer on its own unless the brokerage sets that up for a kind of message, and the brokerage can move that line either way.
- **Fair housing.** Every person gets the same follow-up. Personal details are never used to rank, filter or sort people, it never infers, asks about or acts on a protected characteristic, and a steering question goes to a person.
- **MLS data, as a limit the brokerage writes.** The brokerage writes on its seat which systems the Operator may reach and what it may do in each, and anything not written is refused. A brokerage seat is written to read MLS data only through the brokerage's own licensed feed, with AI use authorized in the way that MLS requires, and never through an agent's login. (From [permission-model.md](permission-model.md), "Rules that hold everywhere".)
- **Licensed work stays with licensed people.** It gives no price or value opinion to a consumer, never negotiates, never advises, and never decides what goes in a contract or what gets signed. Those stay with the agents and the broker.
- **Access codes, as a limit the brokerage writes.** Releasing a lockbox or access code is not written into a brokerage seat's limits, so the Operator is refused it. (From [permission-model.md](permission-model.md), "Rules that hold everywhere".)
- **Money.** It never moves money, never touches earnest money, and sends any question about wire instructions or where to send funds to the brokerage's or the escrow company's verified process.
- **The brokerage holds the controls.** The configuration, the logs and the off switch stay with the brokerage.

Licensed judgment, money, the logs and the off switch come from [industry-packs.md](industry-packs.md), "The rule in every industry". Protected characteristics, steering and wire instructions follow the lines that document states for property management, mortgage, and title and escrow, and moving the sending line either way comes from SMD's Operator page, "Control". MLS data and access codes are limits the brokerage writes on its seat, as the permission model describes. The rest are lines a brokerage seat is configured to hold, drawn from the association sources listed below.

## Systems it connects to

- The brokerage's email, with reading and sending on separate paths where the brokerage's mail system supports it, and sending as an agent only on that agent's approval. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Connections by client system type" and "Email channels".)
- The brokerage's own systems, such as its MLS feed, its transaction management and compliance system, its CRM, its e-signature service and its calendar, each connected the same way as every other system: the vendor's official server, a vetted community server, or one SMD writes, with every tool classified before it can be used. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "How a connection is built" and "Connections by client system type".)
- Events from other systems, routed through the gate. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Other channels".)
- It works on the brokerage's own accounts. (From SMD's Operator page, "Control".)
