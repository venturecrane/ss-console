# Real Estate Use Ideas

This document is a list of ideas for what the Operator could take on at a residential real estate brokerage, grouped by the people whose time it saves: the broker, the agents, the transaction coordinators and office staff, and the brokerage's buyers and sellers. Each idea says what the work looks like today, what the Operator takes on, and the part of the library that capability comes from. Industry facts carry a link to the public sources listed at the end. [real-estate-brokerages.md](real-estate-brokerages.md) describes how a brokerage works and the line the Operator never crosses there, in depth.

## How to use this page

- This page is a list to react to with the broker, not a plan. Read it together, mark the ideas that match real pain, strike the ones that do not, and add the ones it missed.
- Every brokerage runs differently. A brokerage seat is shaped with the brokerage the same way as every other industry pack, and the ideas below are starting points for that conversation.
- Each idea names where its capability comes from in the library, so anyone the broker shows it to can read the source.
- The Operator drafts for people. It never contacts a consumer on its own unless the brokerage sets that up for a kind of message, and the brokerage can move that line either way.
- The ideas that touch licensed work keep that work with licensed people. The section "What stays with licensed people" lists that line.

## For the broker

- **A weekly summary of every open file.** Today the broker learns where files stand by asking agents and coordinators, or by opening each file in the transaction system. The Operator can assemble a summary on a schedule the brokerage sets: files under contract, dates coming up, documents still missing, and files that have gone quiet, sent to the broker to read. (From [permission-model.md](permission-model.md), "Rules that hold everywhere", and [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Connections by client system type".)
- **Supervision and file review, done earlier.** Arizona requires the designated broker to supervise licensees and review transactions, and accepts an electronic review recorded in a history that cannot be altered ([rule](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/arizona/Ariz-Admin-Code-SS-R4-28-1103), [electronic review](https://azre.gov/sites/default/files/2025-07/S.P.S.%202010.01.pdf)). The Operator can check each file against the brokerage's own checklist as documents arrive and bring the broker the gaps while there is still time to fix them. The review and the sign-off stay with the broker. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Connections by client system type".)
- **Coaching and training opportunities from the brokerage's own data.** Brokerages already use reporting built on MLS and back-office data to compare agents' listings, pendings and volume for coaching and goal setting ([back-office reporting](https://blog.brokermint.com/mint/6-ways-to-boost-brokerage-performance-with-reporting-and-analytics)). The Operator can read the brokerage's licensed MLS feed and its back-office system and draft a note to the broker on patterns worth a conversation, such as a new agent with no pendings or files that keep stalling at the same step. The MLS feed is read only through the brokerage's own license, with AI use authorized as that MLS requires, never through an agent's login. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Connections by client system type", and [real-estate-brokerages.md](real-estate-brokerages.md), "The line it never crosses".)
- **Recruiting follow-up.** Agents move between brokerages: in a 2025 study of productive agents across four large MLS regions, annual turnover was 6.8 percent ([agent migration](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/agent-migration-2025-trends/)). The Operator can keep a recruiting list moving: draft the next note to each candidate in the broker's voice, and book the conversation on the broker's calendar. The broker approves each note before it goes. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Email channels" and "Connections by client system type".)
- **Keeping agents, especially new ones.** About 49 percent of agents whose first closing was in 2022 closed nothing in 2023 ([new agent cohorts](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/08/06/fewer-younger-agents-holding-on-amid-market-headwinds)). The Operator can draft check-ins with new agents on the schedule the broker sets and bring the broker anyone who asked for help or went quiet. (From [permission-model.md](permission-model.md), "Rules that hold everywhere".)
- **Standing rules for how things come out.** When the broker, as one of the brokerage's named administrators, says how a kind of output should be shaped, for example that the weekly summary opens with files past a date, that becomes a standing rule once an administrator confirms it. (From [memory.md](memory.md), "Administrator corrections".)
- **One record of what it did.** Every action the Operator takes is logged where the brokerage can see it, which helps when the broker reviews how a file was handled. (From [industry-packs.md](industry-packs.md), "The rule in every industry".)

## For agents

- **Open-house visitor capture and follow-up drafts.** The national association's safety guidance asks that every open-house visitor sign in ([open house safety](https://www.nar.realtor/safety/10-tips-for-holding-a-safe-open-house)), and then the sign-ins need sorting and follow-up. This is the starting preset for a brokerage seat: the Operator captures each visitor and drafts the same follow-up for every one, for the agent to approve. (From [industry-packs.md](industry-packs.md), "Real estate", and [real-estate-brokerages.md](real-estate-brokerages.md), "The line it never crosses".)
- **First response to new inquiries.** Buyers have named responsiveness the trait they most want in an agent ([2014 survey](https://www.redfin.com/blog/2015/03/americans-want-change-in-real-estate.html)). The Operator can read a new inquiry, pull the details that matter and draft the reply in the agent's voice, which goes from the agent's own address once the agent approves it by replying. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Email channels".)
- **Past-client and sphere follow-up.** Most sellers found their agent by referral or used one they had worked with before ([2025 profile takeaways](https://www.nar.realtor/news/economists-outlook/top-10-takeaways-from-nars-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers)). The Operator can keep a schedule of touches with past clients and the agent's sphere, such as a home anniversary note, and draft each one for the agent, with the same cadence for every person on the list. (From [permission-model.md](permission-model.md), "Rules that hold everywhere".)
- **Listing launch coordination.** A listing launch is a sequence of steps that brokerages write down as a process, from the signed listing agreement through photos and the MLS entry to the listing going live ([listing launch practice](https://aare.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/AARE-Listing-Process-and-Best-Practices.pdf)). The Operator can track each step for each new listing, chase what is late, and draft the listing's marketing text and MLS entry for the agent to review. The agent approves what goes live. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Connections by client system type".)
- **Drafts in the agent's own voice.** The voice is written per kind of message from the brokerage's own examples, it sharpens as agents correct drafts, and the brokerage's administrators confirm which corrections become standing rules. (From [memory.md](memory.md), "Voice and format specifications".)
- **Working the way each agent likes.** When an agent says how they want things, such as "send me the summary first", the Operator records that preference for that agent and works that way from then on. (From [memory.md](memory.md), "Learned working preferences".)
- **Reaching it from where the agent works.** Agents can reach the Operator by email, and by the other channels the brokerage chooses, such as chat or texting, which SMD sets up through the same gate. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Other channels".)

## For transaction coordinators and office staff

- **Contract deadline tracking.** An Arizona purchase runs on dates the contract sets: the seller's disclosure delivery, the title review, the inspection period with the buyer's inspection notice and the seller's response, then the loan, the appraisal, the walkthrough and the close of escrow ([purchase contract and escrow](https://www.aaronline.com/?p=78425), [inspection notice](https://www.aaronline.com/?p=79064)). The Operator can keep each file's dates, nudge the right person ahead of each one, and bring the coordinator any date that is close with its step not done. (From [permission-model.md](permission-model.md), "Rules that hold everywhere", and [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Connections by client system type".)
- **Missing-document chasing.** Compliance review runs on a checklist per file, with outstanding items going back to the agent and items still open near closing going to the coordinator and the broker ([compliance checklists](https://enterprisedna.co/resources/guides/real-estate-transaction-compliance-checklists)). The Operator can compare each file in the brokerage's transaction system to the checklist and draft the request for each missing item to the person who owes it. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Connections by client system type".)
- **The buyer agreement before the first showing.** An MLS participant working with a buyer needs a written buyer agreement before touring a home ([written buyer agreements](https://nar.realtor/the-facts/written-buyer-agreements-101)). The Operator can flag a showing on the calendar for a buyer whose file holds no signed agreement and tell the agent before the tour. What the agreement says stays with the agent. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Connections by client system type".)
- **Large file work as a queued job.** Work too long for a reply, such as reviewing every open file against the checklist at once, runs as a queued job with fixed stages, cost limits, an independent review, and proof that the result landed in the brokerage's own system. A new kind of queued job is set up per engagement on the same stages. (From [jobs.md](jobs.md), "Kinds of job" and "From request to delivery".)
- **Status questions answered from the file.** Agents and parties ask the coordinator where a file stands. The Operator can answer the brokerage's own staff from the transaction system and the file's own messages, draft the answer to a buyer or seller for the coordinator to send, and pass anything it cannot answer to the coordinator. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Connections by client system type".)
- **Events from other systems.** When the transaction system, the e-signature service or the showing service reports an event, such as a document signed, it reaches the Operator through the gate and can start the next step. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Other channels".)
- **Files kept where they belong.** Arizona requires transaction records to be kept for a period its statute sets ([record retention](https://www.azleg.gov/ars/32/02151-01.htm)). The Operator works on the brokerage's own systems, and it keeps its own working notes only in folders written for it. (From [memory.md](memory.md), "Record stores".)

## For buyers and sellers

- **Transaction status updates.** Buyers and sellers ask where things stand, and agents ask the coordinator the same thing. The Operator can draft a status update at each step of the file, and for each kind of message the brokerage chooses whether the draft goes to an agent to review or goes out on its own. (From [permission-model.md](permission-model.md), "Ceilings from least to most restrictive".)
- **Showing feedback for sellers.** Feedback from each showing goes first to the listing agent, who passes it to the seller or includes it in a report ([showing feedback](https://www.gaar.com/blog/article/your-client-got-negative-feedback.-now-what)). The Operator can draft the feedback request after each showing, or send it where the brokerage sets that up, gather the answers, and draft a summary for the seller that the listing agent reviews before it goes. (From [permission-model.md](permission-model.md), "Ceilings from least to most restrictive".)
- **Scheduling.** The Operator can propose times for a walkthrough, a signing or a meeting from the agent's calendar and draft the message that offers them. (From [connections-and-channels.md](connections-and-channels.md), "Connections by client system type".)
- **The same follow-up for everyone.** Every buyer and seller gets the same cadence and care. Personal details are never used to rank, filter or sort people. (From [real-estate-brokerages.md](real-estate-brokerages.md), "The line it never crosses".)

## What stays with licensed people

- **Opinions of value.** The Operator gives no price or value opinion to a consumer. It can gather the facts an agent asks for, and the opinion is the agent's.
- **Contract terms and negotiation.** It never decides what goes in a contract or what gets signed, and it never negotiates. It can track dates and documents and draft for the agent.
- **Agency and representation advice.** It never advises a buyer or seller on agency, on a buyer agreement, or on anything else that calls for a licensee. Those questions go to the agent.
- **Fair housing.** It never infers, asks about or acts on a protected characteristic, and a steering question goes to a person.
- **Money and access.** It never moves money or touches earnest money. Releasing a lockbox or access code is not written into a brokerage seat's limits, so it is refused.
- **The broker's review.** The broker's supervision and file sign-off stay with the broker. The Operator brings the broker what it found.

Licensed judgment, money and the record come from [industry-packs.md](industry-packs.md), "The rule in every industry", and from [permission-model.md](permission-model.md), "Rules that hold everywhere". [real-estate-brokerages.md](real-estate-brokerages.md), "The line it never crosses", states the full line.

## Questions to explore

1. Which three ideas on this page would save the most hours in a normal week, and whose hours are they?
2. Which of these ideas does someone in the office already do by hand, and what would they rather do with that time?
3. What would the broker want in a weekly summary, and what would make it worth reading?
4. Which data would show the broker where an agent needs coaching, and how does the brokerage's MLS license describe AI use of that data?
5. What does a recruit or a new agent hear from the brokerage today, and how often?
6. Which messages to buyers and sellers should an agent always approve, and which could go out on their own?
7. Which idea is the right first one to try, and how would the brokerage know it worked?
