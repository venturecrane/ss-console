# Assessment findings - Kessler Built

_Drafted from the assessment conversation on 2026-06-06. This is a working draft for the closing call._

## How Kessler Built runs today

Kessler Built is a residential remodeling business doing kitchens, baths, additions, and full gut jobs, occasionally taking on light commercial. The owner has run it for fifteen years and built it from himself and one other person to about a dozen between employees and trusted subs. Most work comes in by referral. The owner is at the center of nearly everything: estimates, client questions, morning site walkthroughs, material ordering, and scheduling. His wife keeps QuickBooks current.

## What we saw, by area

### Process & how work flows (process_design)

- **The daily plan for each crew runs through the owner's morning walkthrough; when he can't make it, work slows or waits on him.**

  > They said: "Honestly? They call me. Or they wait on something and it slows the day down... if I'm not there in the morning, things move a little slower. I won't pretend otherwise."

- **Job-level detail - client preferences, open decisions, what each job is waiting on - lives with the owner rather than in a written form someone else could run from.** The office whiteboard holds the high-level schedule, but not the per-job specifics.

  > They said: "there's the whiteboard in the office. We've got the schedule up there, what crew is where, what's coming up... But yeah, the detail - the specifics of what a client wants, where we are on a particular decision, what we're waiting on - that's mostly me."

- **The signed-to-started handoff is verbal and carried by the owner rather than documented.** He lines up subs by phone, walks the lead through the plan on site, and handles materials himself.

  > They said: "it's not a formal process with documents flying around everywhere. It's more... I know what needs to happen and I make sure it happens. I communicate it to the right people and we get moving."

- **When one piece of the schedule moves, the owner is the only one who can see how it cascades and reassemble it.** A sub pushing his start a week forced a re-sequence of the following trades.
  > They said: "now I've got a sequencing problem because the flooring was supposed to follow the tile and I've got that crew lined up already and... it cascades. One thing moves and three other things have to move. And I'm the one who has to figure out how all those pieces fit back together."

### Tools & systems (tool_systems)

- **The business runs on three things: QuickBooks, the office whiteboard, and the owner's phone.** He has looked at project management, estimating, and scheduling software and stepped away from it.

  > They said: "Pretty much those three things. Yeah... I've looked at a couple of them. They're either too complicated or they're built for outfits doing commercial work at a different scale than us. Or I'd have to spend three months getting everything set up and I don't have three months."

- **The owner is skeptical that available tools fit his scale, but is open to something that shows job-by-job financial standing without requiring office staff to run it.**
  > They said: "if there was something that could show me job by job where I actually stand financially without me having to build a spreadsheet from scratch... I'd probably look at that. I just don't know if that thing exists in a way that would actually work for how we operate. Everything I've seen assumes you've got a whole office staff running it."

### Visibility into the numbers (data_visibility)

- **The inputs to job profitability live in QuickBooks, but nobody pulls them together job by job to compare actual spend against the original bid.** The owner works from a rough sense and his gut rather than a reconciled number.

  > They said: "if you're asking do I sit down at the end of a job and say 'okay we bid this at X, we actually spent Y, here's what we made' - no. Not really. I know the check cleared, I know we got paid. Whether we got paid enough..."

- **Because that reconciliation doesn't happen, the owner doesn't know with certainty which jobs are his best earners and which quietly cost him.** A large kitchen addition last year is one he believes he may not have netted what he thought.

  > They said: "I don't actually know what I netted on that job. And if I'm honest, I probably couldn't tell you with certainty on most jobs."

- **Change orders are handled informally - sometimes a conversation and a handshake - and the owner sometimes absorbs added cost to protect the client relationship.**
  > They said: "I don't always write those up formally. The changes. Sometimes it's a conversation, a handshake... if I'm being straight with you, sometimes I eat some of it just to keep the peace."

### Customers & pipeline (customer_pipeline)

- **Incoming referrals are captured and followed up through the owner's phone, call history, and memory, not a tracking system.** He can't readily say how many open leads he has or where each stands.

  > They said: "is it in a system somewhere? No. It's in my phone, my call history, maybe a note I jotted down... I couldn't tell you right now how many open leads I've got, where each one stands. That's just honest."

- **Leads have slipped when the owner got busy.**
  > They said: "I've had situations where somebody called, I meant to get back to them, and it slipped. I won't pretend that hasn't happened. You get busy, you're juggling three jobs and a materials problem and a sub who didn't show up, and a call slips through."

### Team & people (team_operations)

- **The trusted-sub bench is thin in every trade, and in at least one trade it is a single person.** Replacing a trusted sub mid-job is hard and time-consuming.

  > They said: "the sub bench thing is even thinner than I made it sound. I've got one electrician I really trust. One. If he's not available I'm in trouble and I know it."

- **The business lost two experienced carpenters in two years; the second departure caught the owner off guard.** They left for larger outfits citing steadier hours.

  > They said: "I lost two good carpenters in the last couple years... Second one caught me off guard. First one I kind of saw coming but I figured he'd work it out, stay. He'd been with me four years."

- **There is no structured conversation to catch retention risk early and no defined path for how a role grows over time.** Carlos, the owner's most relied-on person on site, has been there eight years without that conversation.

  > They said: "there's no path laid out for anybody. No 'you do this for two years and here's what comes next.' Carlos has been with me eight years and I've never really sat down and talked to him about... where he sees himself going. What he wants."

- **Materials sometimes don't arrive when the job needs them.** The owner flagged this as a thread with real weight but the conversation did not go deep on it.
  > They said: "Materials I handle. I know what we need, I'll place the orders, make sure stuff is showing up when it needs to. Sometimes things don't show up when they need to."

## What sits underneath all of it

The owner named, in his own words, a thread that runs beneath the rest: there is no plan for what the business becomes, and much of why everything runs through him traces back to it.

> They said: "there's no plan for what this becomes. Whether I'm doing this exact same thing at sixty, or whether somebody takes it over, or whether I sell it someday... part of why everything runs through me - part of why I never built the systems, never set up a path for Carlos, never wrote down the job knowledge - is because somewhere in the back of my head it was always just _my_ thing... But that's not really a plan. And I'm starting to feel that."

## Where this points

What comes through across both conversations is a real business with a genuine foundation: fifteen years, a referral pipeline, and clients who come back and send others. The strains the owner described - job knowledge held in his head, financials that never get reconciled job by job, leads tracked from memory, a thin bench, and the question of what the business becomes - are the kind of things that are very workable once they're looked at together. None of them require tearing down what already works. The next conversation is where we sit down with the owner, sort through which of these to take on and in what order, and lay out what that would actually look like for Kessler Built specifically. That part happens together, with the picture the owner gave us in front of us.
