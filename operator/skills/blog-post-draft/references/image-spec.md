# Images for blog posts

## What is allowed

1. **The firm's own photographs**, listed in the `seat-reference` store record
   `photos.md`, each with a description and, once uploaded, a WordPress media
   id. These are the first choice.
2. **Licensed stock the firm already holds**, chosen and placed by the
   reviewer. You never download or choose a stock image yourself; you describe
   the image and the reviewer picks it.
3. **No generated images.** This seat does not generate images, and a post
   never says an image is a photograph of something it is not.

## What is never allowed, from any source

- An injury, blood, a wound, a cast or a scar.
- An accident scene: a crashed or damaged vehicle, debris, emergency vehicles
  at a scene, a person on the ground.
- A hospital bed, an ambulance interior, or a wheelchair used to signal injury.
- A person presented as a client, or a caption that says or implies "our
  client". A person in a stock photo is a model; a caption never says who they
  are.
- A check, cash, a dollar figure, or a "won" graphic.
- A real, identifiable crash location tied to a recent news event.

## The image spec (when no firm photo fits)

Two lines the reviewer can act on:

```
Image needed: <the scene, plainly>, landscape, at least 2560 pixels wide, no faces, no damaged vehicles, no injury.
Suggested file name: <lowercase-hyphenated-words>.jpg
```

Good subjects: a road sign, a crosswalk in daylight, a painted bicycle lane, a
calendar or papers on a desk, a courthouse exterior, the office exterior.

## Alt text and caption

- **Alt text** describes what the image shows, in one sentence under 125
  characters, for a reader who cannot see it. It does not sell anything and
  does not name the firm unless the firm's sign is in the picture.
- **Caption** is one plain line about the post's subject. It never says "our
  client", never names a person, never carries a result.
