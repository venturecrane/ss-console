# Blog post format

## Shape

- **Title**: plain, specific, under 65 characters, the reader's question or the
  subject in their words. No claim about the firm, no exclamation mark, no
  ALL-CAPS.
- **Excerpt**: one or two sentences, under 160 characters, saying what the post
  explains. Used as the WordPress excerpt and usually as the search snippet.
- **Body**: 600 to 1,000 words. Short paragraphs (two to four sentences). Two to
  four `<h2>` sections. A bulleted list where the content is a list. Link to
  primary sources (the statute, the agency, the court opinion) with `<a href>`;
  no links to other law firms.
- **The firm line**: one sentence in the body that names the firm and its office
  city exactly as the registers give them, usually in the closing paragraph:
  "<Firm name> is a personal injury firm in <office city>."
- **Closing**: a general invitation is allowed ("If you have questions about how
  this applies to you, a lawyer can explain your options"). Never "tell us about
  your accident in the comments", never "call now", never a promise.
- **Disclaimer**: the firm's authored blog disclaimer (`disclosures.blog`), word
  for word, as the last paragraph. If the post mentions a result, the results
  sentence too; if it mentions fees, the costs sentence too.

## HTML

Allowed: `<p>`, `<h2>`, `<h3>`, `<ul>`, `<ol>`, `<li>`, `<a href>`, and
`<strong>` sparingly inside the post (never in the email). No inline styles, no
scripts, no images inside the body (the featured image carries the picture).

## Search without stuffing

- The words a reader would search appear in the title, the first paragraph and
  one `<h2>`, naturally.
- One topic per post. If the slate topic is really two, say so to the reviewer
  rather than writing both into one post.
- Mention the office city where it is true and useful (local roads, local
  courts), never as a list of towns.

## Spanish posts

Only when the reviewer asks. Every disclosure (the firm line, the blog
disclaimer, and any results or costs sentence) appears in Spanish, taken from
the registers' `es` text. If the registers carry no Spanish text for a
disclosure the post needs, draft nothing and say so.
