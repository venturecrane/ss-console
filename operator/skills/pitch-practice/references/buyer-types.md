# Buyer Types

The kinds of buyer a representative meets, for practice and drills. Each one matches a buyer scenario in scenarios.yaml. Product facts come only from the product library; the library sections named here are where to read before talking to that buyer. No industry, business or person is named.

## The owner who signs

Runs a busy business, has been pitched software many times, and signs every agreement personally. Cares about what the business gets, what could go wrong, and how to stop if it does not work out. Lead with the outcome for the business and the managed service. Read: what-it-is.md, "Definition"; permission-model.md, "Pause, stop and safety"; what-smd-commits-to.md, "Ending the service, return and deletion".

## The office administrator who will live with it

Keeps the inbox, the calendar and the records moving, and already has too many tools. Will be the first to correct it. Cares about not learning one more system, whether corrections stick, and whether anything goes out without being seen. Lead with nothing new to log in to and learned working preferences. Read: how-the-operator-works.md, "Before and after: what changes for the business"; memory.md, "Learned working preferences".

## The security or IT reviewer

Signs off before anything new connects to the business's accounts, reads terms closely, and tests every claim. Cares about isolation, who can see what, credentials and the record. Lead with precision and never overclaim. Read: isolation-and-data-custody.md, "Isolation between clients", "What SMD can see beyond the console" and "The audit record".

## The senior staff member who fears replacement

Holds the knowledge the business runs on and worries the work is aimed at them. Cares about blame for mistakes, who decides what it may do, and whether it keeps notes on them. Lead with their people directing and judging the work, and the client writing the limits. Read: how-the-operator-works.md, "One worker, configured for one business"; permission-model.md, "Ceilings from least to most restrictive".

## The buyer who already bought a point tool

Pays for a chat assistant or a single-purpose tool already, and may have bought one that nobody used. Cares about overlap and adoption. Lead with the Operator connecting to what they have and covering the work between tools. Read: what-it-is.md, "Definition"; connections-and-channels.md, "Other channels".

## The procurement-minded buyer

Asks for terms before anything else: data ownership, model training, incident notice, service levels, liability and exit. Cares about what is written down. Lead with the agreement's standard commitments, and bring anything about price or a custom term to SMD. Read: what-smd-commits-to.md, "Who owns the data" and "What SMD does not promise".

## Using buyer types in practice

Ask to practice with a buyer type by name, for example "let's practice, the security reviewer", or ask for a drill on a theme, for example "drill me on trust". The industry example scenario is one illustration of the same model in one kind of business; it is never a promise to any other business.
