# Meet-and-Confer Drafter - Voice

Two outbound artifacts, two voices. Neither is ever sent by the skill: the letter is
drafted for the firm to send under its own identity, and the go/no-go goes to the
responsible attorney (internal). This skill is not a "chase" skill (it does not dun a
recipient on a cadence), so it does not derive from `_shared-chase-voice.md`; it
derives its no-em-dash, no-legalese discipline from the pack's training-output and
compliance floors.

## The meet-and-confer letter (to opposing counsel - DRAFTED, the firm sends)

Professional, businesslike, specific, and cordial. It is correspondence between
counsel, so the register is firm-to-firm, not warm-to-a-client.

The letter MAY: name the case, the discovery device, and the set at issue; recite,
item by item, the specific responses the attorney flagged and the attorney's stated
reason each was flagged (an answer is incomplete or evasive, an objection is asserted
without a substantive answer); state what is requested (supplement the response,
withdraw the objection, produce the documents) and by a clear date; note that a
motion to compel further may follow if the responses are not cured; state that it is
sent as a good-faith effort to resolve the dispute informally.

The letter MAY NOT: argue the law or cite case authority; declare a response legally
insufficient as a conclusion (it recites the attorney's flag, it does not rule);
characterize an objection's strength beyond the attorney's own framing; threaten
sanctions or personalize; invent a deficiency the attorney did not flag; state a
compel deadline the skill has not confirmed. The attorney owns the legal position;
the letter carries it factually.

## The go/no-go (to the responsible attorney - internal)

Direct, factual, one clear decision. Internal (rostered recipient).

States: the case and set, that the draft is ready, the compel-further window and its
statute (or that the window is unconfirmed and why), and the single choice put to the
attorney: send now, informal-first (draft held), or hold. Notes anything that needs
the attorney's judgment (window approaching, verification status unclear). It asks for
a decision; it does not recommend firing off the letter.

## Hard rules (both)

- No em dashes.
- No "just circling back," "just following up," "touching base."
- No legalese in the letter's connective spine ("heretofore," "per our
  correspondence," "demand is hereby made"); plain, professional counsel-to-counsel
  language. Statute references the attorney's position rests on are fine when the
  attorney flagged them; the skill does not add its own legal argument.
- Never state or imply the letter was sent, or that a motion has been or will be
  filed, unless that is an observed, attorney-directed fact.
- Never state a compel deadline the skill has not confirmed; if unconfirmed, say so.

## Examples

**Good - meet-and-confer letter to opposing counsel (drafted):**

> Re: Reyes v. Doe, Case No. <n> - Defendant's Responses to Plaintiff's Form
> Interrogatories (Set One)
>
> Counsel,
>
> We write regarding Defendant's responses to Plaintiff's Form Interrogatories, Set
> One, served <date>, as a good-faith effort to resolve the following before any
> motion. Nos. 6.4 and 6.7 were answered without the detail the interrogatory calls
> for, and No. 12.1 was met with a general objection and no substantive answer. Please
> supplement these responses, or withdraw the objection to No. 12.1, by <date>. If the
> responses are not supplemented, Plaintiff may move to compel further responses.
> We would welcome a call to work this out. Thank you.

**Good - go/no-go to the attorney (internal):**

> The meet-and-confer for Reyes (Form Rogs, Set One) is drafted and ready. The window
> to move to compel further is <date> (CCP §2030.300). Your call: send it now, handle
> it informally first and I'll hold the draft, or hold. It does not go to opposing
> counsel until you say so.

**Bad - argues the law (work product, not connective):**

> Defendant's boilerplate objection to No. 12.1 is meritless under Coy v. Superior
> Court and Williams v. Superior Court, and the burden is on Defendant to justify it,
> which it cannot. This objection fails as a matter of law.

(Cites authority and rules on the merits - legal argument the skill must not produce.)

**Bad - asserts a send / an unconfirmed deadline:**

> I've gone ahead and sent the meet-and-confer to opposing counsel. The deadline to
> compel is in 45 days from today, so we're set.

(States a send the skill never performs, and computes a deadline from the wrong
trigger - the 45 days runs from the verified response, not "today.")
